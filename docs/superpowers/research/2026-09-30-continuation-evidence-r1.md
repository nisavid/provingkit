# Continuation capability and local-history evidence

This investigation gives Praxis's decision owners a catalog of encountered continuation failures and working techniques, a surface-specific native capability comparison, and a qualification design for choosing an adaptive return path. The common requirement is effort-level reconciliation: after an operation or worker returns, its owner checks the requested outcome, remaining obligations, current authority, evidence, and next useful action. Native turn completion alone does not supply that decision.

The local histories support this requirement and several competing return techniques. Current primary documentation and exposed contracts identify where in-turn waits, result notifications, peer messages, and scheduling may supply a return. Useful resumed work, restart recovery, observation quality, and economic advantage still require separate evidence. This research informs the existing procedure, adapter, evaluation, and adoption decisions; it selects no reusable cadence or operating threshold and installs no procedure.

These findings favor comparing a route driven by native events with an adaptive deadline fallback where the exact harness supports both. Neither a configured timer nor a successful send proves those claims.

### Comparison and implications

**Inference: active waits are the shortest supported path while the coordinator is working.** They can collect several children without scheduling an additional periodic work turn. They still end with the foreground turn or process, so an active wait cannot by itself be the return path after the coordinator has yielded. A child's completion, a delivery notice, and an effort's completion are different events.

**Inference: native event delivery is promising where both endpoints expose the required lifecycle.** Claude's documented task notifications and local main-session idle notices make that possibility concrete. Codex exposes explicit follow-ups and result waits here. The evidence does not establish a portable mechanism that converts every idle worker or completed child into a useful new coordinator turn. The same capability name on two surfaces is insufficient; bind endpoint, executor version, relationship, transport, and receiver evidence.

**Inference: a scheduled return can cover a quiet interval, but its cost depends on actionable returns.** It is valuable when no reliable event route remains, and requires separate evidence for configuration, wake delivery, reconciliation, and resumed work. A fixed frequent poll can repeatedly consume a coordinator turn without changing any obligation. An adaptive fallback is a candidate for evaluation, with its interval/backoff owned by the existing decision tickets; the current thirty-minute stopgap is not a settled reusable threshold.

**Inference: reconciliation is necessary on every uncertain route.** The coordinator should distinguish a completed work turn from a finished obligation and inspect the receiver before replaying an uncertain continuation. Restart can restore a transcript, a scheduled configuration, or neither; it does not establish that the previous send was acted on. A workflow cursor and episode/claim state help avoid redoing known work, but their persistence and ordering require evidence.

The settled [Praxis model](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/157#issuecomment-5745374858) requires every obligation to be satisfied, waived, or transferred before effort completion, with no active claim/handoff or unknown effects. The [stewardship procedure decision](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/144#issuecomment-5745003755) keeps Praxis coordination, Rolecasting dispatch, and Tricritical review distinct. This research supplies facts for [procedure roster](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/158), [adapter selection](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/159), [evaluation qualification](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/160), and [adoption](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/161). It does not select or install their answer. Aeon Bell's existing external-condition scope should not become a generic unfinished-work scanner through a research conclusion. Its [native ordering decision](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/97#issuecomment-5742801195) still needs to govern its own implementation and qualification.

### Proposed measurement protocol

This is a concrete future measurement design for the existing owners, not an authorization to run it or a selected pass threshold.

1. **Bind a candidate and its evidence.** Record the procedure revision, adapter, controller and worker surfaces, exact executors, account route without secret values, relationship, applicable permissions, and schedule/event configuration. Record the supported obligation and what counts as useful progress before observing it. Use harmless documentary work and disposable cases.
2. **Separate each stage.** Timestamp the actionable obligation, worker state/change, event or due time, controller observation, continuation decision, dispatch acceptance, receiver-origin work turn, first useful result, reconciliation, and obligation closeout. Retain message/task/episode correlation and native status before and after. Mark missing timestamps as missing; do not derive delivery latency from a send response alone.
3. **Compare matched routes.** Observe in-turn native waits, an eligible native event route, and one durable fallback under the same work and states. Keep dispatcher selection and model/effort constant where possible. Report both successful returns and unsuccessful opportunities, with denominators, rather than only examples that happened to work.
4. **Exercise lifecycle uncertainty in a separately authorized qualification.** Include an idle coordinator, busy receiver, child completion while the parent is idle, controller/app restart, temporarily unavailable target, a lost send response, and overlapping schedule updates. Establish whether recovery consults durable state and the receiver before retry. Record whether resumed work repeats a completed obligation. This report does not claim those scenarios have been run.
5. **Use human waits as negative controls.** Include a pending operator answer, an explicit pause, a canceled task, and already completed work. Independently actionable preparation beside a held decision is a positive case. Verify that the procedure preserves the actual human boundary and does not classify every idle session as a failure.
6. **Measure cost against useful changes.** Record model input/output and cached tokens where exposed, tool calls, scheduler runs, unchanged return turns, elapsed active/wait time, actual useful results, and duplicate work. Distinguish the tool's zero-work observation from a model turn needed to interpret it. Where the native source lacks usage data, say so; use version/date-bound published pricing only if later owners choose a monetary comparison. Do not substitute shorter replies for measured lower cost.
7. **Assess adaptive behavior.** Record what event or observation chooses the next return time, how unchanged runs back off, what restores a shorter interval, when the fallback stops, and what happens after restart. Evaluate one coordinator fallback versus per-worker recurring jobs as separate candidates. Consolidated waits, bounded reads, quiet unchanged returns, and no redundant schedules are proposed controls, not proven savings.
8. **Report a tied result.** Publish the exact candidate revision, repeatable configuration, scenarios, observation coverage, failures, uncertainty, and aggregate cost/latency distributions. Owners choose adequacy thresholds and evaluate both false resumptions and missed useful returns. Renew the result when the candidate, executor, or evidence dependencies change. Capture the reviewed procedure and downstream invocation only after its owning decisions accept it.

### How candidates were examined

The automated breadth scout tagged conversational records for orchestration, waits, messaging, listening/callbacks, monitoring/cadence, recovery/handoff, gates, continuation, and complaints. Harness-provided instruction catalogs were excluded from conversational keyword classification where recognizable. Separate full-corpus tool scouting indexed 1,214,149 tool-call/output records and found nested continuation calls inside orchestration cells as well as direct calls. Tool-name matches identify recorded routes, not verified successful invocations. Encrypted or opaque fields were not decoded; text unavailable to the scout can therefore conceal additional semantic episodes.

Keyword hits were not classified as failures. The refinement retained 41 short complaint candidates, 54 broader main-session candidates, and 45 main-session mechanism candidates; these sets overlap and contain instructions, relay messages, synthetic tests, irrelevant lexical matches, and repeated context. Forty-two bundles were read in surrounding context, including initiating instructions, before/after assistant messages, available native receipts, and selected receiver records. This catalog does not assert that every broad keyword hit received full contextual review. The three refined scouts contain 128 distinct references. Every one received a retained disposition: included, duplicate, instruction-only, irrelevant, or insufficient context. The private ledger preserves the reason and source identity for each. No refined candidate remains unexamined. Broad keyword matches outside these refinements remain scouting evidence rather than classified failures; no method can establish that a lexical scout found every semantic episode.

Evidence labels below have these meanings:

- **Observed:** the retained record contains the event, message, tool response, or transition stated. It does not by itself prove an external side effect.
- **Reported:** a participant describes an external result, unavailable receiver state, or cause that was not independently verified in this survey.
- **Inferred:** the sequence supports a plausible explanation; competing explanations remain.
- **Proposed:** a useful comparison or procedural implication, awaiting its owning decision and qualification.

### Recurring patterns and competing explanations

The strongest recurring pattern is premature return at a boundary smaller than the requested outcome: commit, draft publication, pending checks, an operation handoff, or a worker's correction horizon. CH01, CH03–CH08, CH19, and CH20 contain operator intent and subsequent continuation that support that interpretation. The remedy implied by these cases is explicit reconciliation of the unfinished effort, not merely longer waits. A scheduler cannot supply a missing next-owner decision or correct a mistaken claim that readiness belongs to a human.

CH32 adds a completion-criterion mismatch: a plan was called implemented when execution was requested. CH34 adds a reported completion-identity error in a dispatcher. Those require different corrections from simply scheduling another prompt.

The second pattern is overinterpretation of observations. CH02 directly demonstrates empty native read projections beside substantive receiver records; CH18 shows a worker already waiting despite a “stalled” narrative. Missing native items, a UI label, or sender completion must remain evidence about that observation surface. Plausible explanations include projection lag or omission, a bounded worker returning a gate, delayed delivery, a process failure, or an actual abandoned next step. This survey does not choose one common cause for all such cases.

A third pattern is mismatched lifetime. Operation completion stopped continued listening in CH10; restart discarded volatile delivery evidence in CH11; archival removed a resume checkout in CH06; and a human wait outlived a session-bound heartbeat in CH24. Shared process fate is reported in CH25. These mechanisms interact: a successful send may lack an acknowledgment, a preserved artifact may lack a live session, and a completed worker may leave open obligations. Durable identity and recoverable evidence are useful, but they do not themselves make a route runnable.

CH36 shows a separate replay risk: retries without durable item outcomes can repeatedly request already-refused work. CH37 separates discoverable startup instructions from an actual idle wake source. CH30 shows that a confirmed hold needs a current roster and a confirmed release, and CH29 shows that resource admission can prevent a coordinator from continuing while children have already returned.

Counterexamples prevent a “wake anything idle” policy. CH13 has an explicit standby, CH14 has reserved push ownership, CH12 has a pause plus pending decisions, and CH15 must preserve a human answer surface. CH08 expressly excludes merge. Conversely, CH04 demonstrates useful scheduled continuation, CH18 demonstrates a working join, CH20 demonstrates in-turn review continuation, and CH21 demonstrates independent progress during a real wait. None establishes a reliability percentage, optimal cadence, or general operating guarantee.

An economical comparison should start with the cheapest trustworthy evidence of changed prerequisites. Event-driven receipt/result reconciliation is a candidate when the native surface can reliably deliver and replay it. In-turn waits are useful while the coordinator can still make progress or await a bounded result.
