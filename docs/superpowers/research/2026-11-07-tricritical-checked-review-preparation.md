# Preparing a usable Tricritical review

A usable ordinary review needs a complete prospective review input and a closed dispatch plan before critics start, followed by native observations and complete reports as they occur. The maintained instructions already assign those checks; the alpha.7 incident shows that the calling coordinator did not supply all of them. The available record does not establish whether the coordinator failed to discover the instructions, understood them differently, or used a helper that appeared to check more than it does. A checked preparation route may help, but the incident does not establish that a new generic launcher is necessary.

This investigation supports [the checked-review map](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/547) and its [research question](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/901). Maintained-source references below are pinned to repository revision `54e2ca8987a1d0f4fa2ac2da273681aed15abdbd`; installed copies of the invocation boundary and native freeze helper matched the respective source bytes during this investigation. The incident's private origin records remain with the project coordinator; the public map records the reported outcomes and does not purport to authenticate them.

The coordinator proposed checking the complete plan before launch. That is a reasonable hypothesis. The observed failures do not yet distinguish missing guidance, missed invocation, ambiguous ownership, or insufficient mechanical enforcement as the root cause.

Ordinary review uses a live same-leader plan, individual selection records, controller observations, raw reports, and verification under the recorded minima. It does not require Task Witness or authenticated receipts by default. Requested model and read-only authority are not product attestations. A consumer that selects stronger assurance still has to obtain it; ordinary records cannot substitute. See the [ordinary and witnessed boundaries](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/blob/54e2ca8987a1d0f4fa2ac2da273681aed15abdbd/plugins/tricritical/skills/review/references/invocation-boundary.md).

## Options for the decision

1. **Improve discovery and explicit caller use of the existing contracts.** The leader constructs the current increment, review input, closed dispatch plan, and selection records using maintained Rolecasting and Tricritical instructions. This preserves the existing ordinary route and adds little equipment. It depends on caller compliance; this incident shows the risk of relying on prose alone, but does not prove which guidance was actually consulted.
2. **Provide a shared preparation template or producer.** A maintained entry point could make the required semantic fields and phase transitions visible, while still letting Rolecasting own topology and model selection and Tricritical own review completeness. This could reduce omission without a new operator approval. A template alone does not guarantee the actual native tool call or complete final handoff uses the prepared record.
3. **Add a checked prospective contract at the actual dispatch consumer.** A narrowly scoped validator could reject missing, duplicate, extra, stale, mismatched, unauthorized, or postdated prospective bindings before the first critic starts and bind the dispatch to the validated plan. Its contract must not require future child IDs, terminal results, or critic reports at preparation time. A separate return check must preserve original reports and native observations after execution. This offers stronger mechanical prevention, but adds implementation, schema and caller-integration costs; a standalone checker not on the real path can be bypassed in the same way as a neglected instruction.
4. **Use the existing terminal bundle validator.** It can be useful for consumers that explicitly select its authenticated retained-evidence contract. It is not a substitute for the prospective ordinary path and cannot produce absent issuer evidence. Requiring it for every ordinary review would add a different assurance requirement rather than fix the demonstrated caller gap.

The choice belongs to [the correction decision](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/902). Whichever route is selected should exercise the real caller: reject an incomplete plan before a critic starts, allow a valid ordinary review to reach a usable complete handback, preserve actual failure and raw reports, and invalidate only scopes whose candidate or dependency identities change. Do not fabricate a prospective record after dispatch or equate a hash with proof that its contents were checked. The current evidence does not select a particular implementation owner; the leader owns the cross-boundary integration, Rolecasting owns its plan and model-selection contracts, Tricritical owns review inputs and completeness, and downstream consumers retain their own acceptance gates.
