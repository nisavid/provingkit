## Critique disposition

**Retain the historical correction; narrow its implication.** H1 presents affirmative scaffold and control evidence for all three repositories. It refutes a simple configured/unconfigured account, but cannot determine that every present difference is intentional or desirable. Its bounded search did not find a general configuration procedure or rationale for every difference; that is a coverage gap, not proof that the knowledge never existed. The reported solo-maintainer approval decision and retained-history freeze are especially useful because they explain apparently permissive or obstructive settings through purpose. They are examples to reason from, not defaults to inherit. [H1, Creation and configuration sequence; What is retained, what is missing]

**Retain G1's mechanism distinctions; defer its schema.** G1 supplies source-backed distinctions among effective rules, authored rules, actor eligibility, check producer/revision/event, and scanner execution versus coverage. These strengthen the domain model. Its long setting-record field list and eight relationship types are useful research prompts, but the evidence does not establish that every catalog entry needs them, that each deserves a machine-readable relation, or that a catalog is the eventual component boundary. Treat them as questions a supported decision may need to answer. [G1, Effective rules and merge behavior; Proposed shape for brief guidance and its catalog]

**Keep the external comparison bounded.** The four reviewed approaches separate configuration writing, inheritance, assessment, and explanation in useful ways. The finding that none of those reviewed sources establishes the complete desired advisory behavior is not evidence that no existing solution could support it, or that a new engine must be built. A later panel should compare reuse at the needed interface, including who controls fields and what authority deployment requires. [G1, Existing approaches: reuse candidates and limits]

**Correct one enforcement formulation.** O1 calls its active rules and twelve required checks “substantial enforcement.” I recommend “substantial active merge requirements” there. O1 expressly did not run a negative fixture; active configuration and documented semantics support the intended requirement, but do not establish that every named check blocks the intended failure. This is a wording correction, not a finding that protection is absent. [O1, Merge and review controls; Evidence limits]

**Do not upgrade the historical canary.** H1 reports a code-scanning blocker while an unresolved conversation was also blocking. That supports the recorded observations, but does not isolate code scanning as the cause of inability to merge. A later distinguishing test needs all unrelated gates satisfied, the intended gate varied, and the actual acceptance decision observed. Neither lane claims that test has been performed. [H1, Both products: a separate, tested hardening increment]

**Retain source-fit questions without prescribing hardening.** O1 distinguishes repository-level pinning policy from already pinned source workflows, and missing required-check names from proven coverage gaps. Those are important corrections to checkbox reasoning. Requiring the omitted names may be useful, redundant, or insufficient; checking the claims those jobs establish comes first. The CodeQL response similarly establishes a configuration observation, not a security verdict. [O1, Workflow and security controls]

Agreement among lanes on these distinctions is not independent runtime validation. The exchange resolves conceptual overclaims and improves the next questions; it does not qualify GitHub behavior or accept repository policy.

## What changed in the conceptual alternatives

My earlier A, B, and C remain **comparison candidates**, not architecture recommendations:

- **A, concern-indexed casebook:** still attractive for contextual rationale. I no longer presume it sufficient. G1's inherited rules and check conclusion/event distinctions create credible retrieval failures that a few friendly examples may conceal. It must pass cases requiring a consequential relation that the initial request does not name.
- **B, sparse relationships:** gains a stronger reason to be tested, especially for dependent check identities, inherited restrictions, and conflicting settings. It does not earn a general solver, exhaustive graph, or fixed relation vocabulary. The authoring burden and false confidence from missing relations remain unmeasured.
- **C, behavior-first scenarios:** gains value as a way to challenge both alternatives. H1's check migration and confounded canary show why configuration readback is insufficient. Yet scenarios cannot be the only knowledge source: explaining eligibility, ownership, or an intentional freeze may require policy and source evidence before any test is sensible.

The useful minimum is therefore a **content obligation**, not a storage schema: for each supported recommendation, show the concern, applicable circumstances, suggested choice and alternatives, consequential interactions, rationale, source/observation limits, and the evidence needed for its behavior claim. Add detail when it changes a decision. Do not require every entry to repeat platform entitlement, rollback, or event semantics when irrelevant; do not omit them when decisive. This replaces D1's untested “casebook first” preference with a fair comparison on shared evidence.

## User choice needs separate judgments

D1's proposed categories mixed recommendation strength, permissibility, and uncertainty. That could accidentally make an agent's preference look like a prohibition. I now propose separate questions:

1. What does the user want, including deliberate deviations from recommendations?
2. What applicable policy actually constrains the choice, and who established it?
3. What consequence or harm is evidenced, inferred, or unresolved?
4. What recommendation follows, and what authority exists to act?

These are reasoning duties, not prescribed fields. A preference disagreement alone should not prevent an arbitrary user configuration. A sourced platform limitation can make a requested outcome unavailable without making the user's preference illegitimate. Established-policy incompatibility and actively harmful behavior require different explanations; neither should be inferred from distance to a recommended baseline.

One important counterexample comes from H1: a no-bypass freeze can deliberately prevent changes. Thus “this setup has no working merge path” is an observation to explain against intent, not automatically a defect or harm. Likewise, a positive approval requirement with no eligible reviewer could be an accidental deadlock or an intended temporary hold. G1 establishes relevant mechanism questions; H1 supplies a real reason not to presume liveness is always the overriding objective. [H1, Provingkit: deliberate cutover governance; G1, Reviews, freshness, and one-person operation]

The definition and treatment of active harm remain stakeholder decisions. Uncertainty should trigger a narrow question about the consequential unknown, not a fabricated permanent rule. Confirmation should show the concrete recommended set and material deviations while respecting existing authority; it should not turn every retrieved caveat into another approval prompt.

## Freshness and actual enforcement

G1's public-preview changes, product-variant distinctions, and API-version caveat strengthen the need to bind claims to their sources. H1 shows a different freshness problem: a consumer can hold an earlier procedure after the producer corrects it. O1 shows a third: source workflow content and current repository settings are different evidence surfaces. One generic “last checked” timestamp cannot stand for all three. [G1, Research boundary and evidence; H1, Retained procedure and the DCO correction; O1, Workflow and security controls]

For a supported recommendation, the later design should be able to recover the mechanism source, the policy reason, the target observations, the procedure revision consumed, and any observed behavior result. Which of these must refresh depends on the claim and change. A new procedure revision may invalidate a recommendation without changing platform behavior; a new repository state may invalidate application assumptions while the catalog remains sound. No uniform refresh interval is selected here.

The minimum enforcement challenge is causal: can the intended path proceed when it should, and is the intended failure actually refused under the specified actor, revision, and event? A matching check name, enabled scanner, successful API write, or aggregate assessment score cannot individually answer that. Test costs and authorization matter; where execution is unavailable, the result must remain source-backed or unqualified rather than being promoted to observed success.

## Owner and consumer seams

H1's DCO example makes the abstract interface concrete: a maintained procedure, a consumer's immutable binding, the consumer's local policy and allowed difference, and an actual check-producer identity. It supports preserving those distinctions. It does **not** assign all repository-configuration work to Sacrysty, transfer that procedure, or select a new shared owner. [H1, Retained procedure and the DCO correction; cited [merged procedure](https://github.com/nisavid/sacrysty/blob/3dd23c4f53da47f83bdf7a82cc98094a4f574501/docs/agents/dco-provisioning.md)]

Useful later comparisons should ask whether a consumer can locate the reviewed revision, declare a local deviation without copying the procedure, discover a material correction, and avoid a second writer fighting existing configuration management. The interface should expose authority and field ownership only to the extent needed for the operation. It need not become a new ownership framework.

Local alpha deployment must eventually show that the selected equipment can be found and invoked in a supported harness, using its intended maintained source and revision. That evidence is distinct from hosted GitHub enforcement evidence. The implementation map should preserve both claims if both are selected, rather than using either as a substitute for the other. [M1, Architecture controls]

## Distinguishing cases for the architecture panel

These proposed cases adapt the exchanged evidence; they are not new observed outcomes. Give every competing design the same facts and policies, then compare consequential omissions, false prohibitions, explanation quality, maintenance effort, and recovery from changed evidence.

| Case | Question that distinguishes designs |
| --- | --- |
| Sole-maintainer approval versus intentional freeze | Does the design ask about purpose and eligibility, or mechanically repair both toward a working merge path? |
| Same check label, changed producer | Does it preserve producer identity and consumer binding without confusing a display name with evidence? |
| Passing conclusion from a skipped or irrelevant event path | Can it explain the check's actual promise and identify what still requires qualification? |
| Repository setting relaxed while an inherited rule remains | Does it distinguish the proposed local value from the effective restriction and explain who can change it? |
| Workflow already pinned; repository policy permits unpinned actions | Does it explain the difference without claiming that existing source is unpinned or that future source is constrained? |
| Consumer's reviewed procedure corrected upstream | Can it identify which downstream decision needs reconsideration without invalidating unrelated work? |
| Two blockers in an enforcement experiment | Does it withhold a causal claim and propose the smallest isolated comparison? |
| Unsupported or unavailable observation | Does it preserve unknown/unavailable rather than silently recording disabled or treating the user choice as harmful? |

The first panel need not exercise every case live. Paper scenarios can distinguish reasoning and interface choices; later authorized fixtures can distinguish executable behavior. Acceptance thresholds and supported contexts remain decisions.

## Map implications and remaining decisions

The research map should now retain the qualified historical correction, the source/readback/enforcement distinctions, the unsettled harm boundary, and these distinguishing cases. It should not encode the long catalog schema or A/B/C as accepted architecture. The separate design panel must consume the frozen research revision, compare independently developed alternatives, and refresh the map as findings change. Before that panel ends, Wayfinder must chart an implementation path to maintained source, current review and behavior evidence, authorized publication, local alpha deployment, and verified invocation. These are handoff requirements from the amendment, not work completed by this lane. [M1]

The remaining stakeholder decisions are the first useful capability and supported repository contexts; the meaning and handling of active harm; which policy sources may constrain choices; the desired balance of human and agent review; and which qualification costs are justified for the selected alpha. Ownership and equipment form belong in the separate architecture/design decision. That panel should begin with these evidence obligations and counterexamples, not a preselected catalog engine.
