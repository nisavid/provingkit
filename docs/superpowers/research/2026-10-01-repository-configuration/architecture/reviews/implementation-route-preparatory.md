## Actionable findings on the original drafts

### 1. Assign qualification evidence by stage before implementation can close

**Evidence:** `implementation-map/implementation.md:3,9` requires satisfying the qualification contract and exercising the **full** qualification contract. `implementation-map/qualification-contract.md:9` includes installed identity and useful fresh invocation. The supplied graph blocks publication/deployment on implementation and fresh invocation on deployment. `implementation.md:11` also correctly limits its result to a source candidate, exposing the inconsistency.

**Effect:** The edges are acyclic, but their acceptance obligations are not executable in that order if “full” is taken literally. Implementation cannot establish the final installed-candidate and fresh-invocation evidence before the task permitted to deploy that candidate can begin. Alternatively, closing implementation as fully qualified would overstate its evidence. Temporary source evaluation can establish the procedure's pre-deployment behavior, but it does not discharge an installed-revision claim without the selected deployment and fresh-client observations.

**Correction:** Have the qualification contract assign each obligation to its owning stage. Implementation closes with current review and all pre-deployment source/behavior obligations satisfied. Publication/deployment owns published and installed identity and supported discovery observations. The final consumer task owns useful fresh invocation and any remaining selected local-client obligations. Keep any hosted-fixture enforcement claims with the stage that actually observes them. The final map closeout verifies all required obligations on the same final candidate. Do not add a reverse dependency or require the producer to consume its own final deployment receipt.

### 2. Keep work beyond the selected alpha outside this map's fog

**Evidence:** `implementation-map/map.md:3` fixes a locally deployed GitHub alpha as the destination. Its `Not yet specified` section at lines 23–24 includes a later Provingkit assessment and “broader live qualification” or “additional providers” for a “useful next increment.” Wayfinder defines fog as unresolved work toward this map's destination; later increments belong outside it.

**Effect:** These entries can silently graduate into more alpha blockers or expand the destination after the selected workflows and local deployment are already complete. The user asked for a separate delivery map, not an open-ended improvement program.

**Correction:** After the pending choices settle the first-alpha contexts and clients, put additional contexts, providers, and later repository-control initiatives in `Out of scope` with a brief successor note. Keep only investigations necessary to substantiate the selected alpha's claims in fog. Richer knowledge and operation helpers may remain available as in-scope corrective choices when actual alpha failures warrant them; they should not become independent expansion goals or compulsory architecture changes. A later Provingkit assessment remains follow-up unless the operator explicitly selects it as an alpha use case or qualification target.

## Scope that is sound in the reviewed route

- The proposed source and qualification contracts can proceed independently after the named design prerequisites, and implementation waits for both. Nothing in the reviewed texts requires a native dependency between those two contract tickets.
- Publication/deployment followed by useful fresh invocation is the right direction. The distinct final consumer task prevents an installed listing or copied file from being mistaken for useful operation.
- The map explicitly overrides Wayfinder's planning-only default for its future delivery tasks while retaining the present charting-only hold. It is separate from the design map, rather than its child. No dependency on closure of the entire design map was proposed, so creating the delivery map before design closeout does not itself introduce a cycle.
- The source contract accounts for both supported packaging choices without selecting one. It requires a concrete source-to-global route for the standalone Skill and separate identity for an independent Plugin, and preserves the current Kit's membership and default route.
- Maintained source, behavioral evidence, published artifacts, installed state, fresh discovery, useful invocation, and hosted enforcement are explicitly separated. Native tool use is allowed for uncataloged settings; the draft does not demand a universal settings catalog, custom actuator per operation, repeated approval for existing authority, or a hardening score.
- The qualification draft names complete examples of all five accepted workflows, deliberate choices, authority uncertainty, existing writers, conflicting evidence, no-op and partial effects, and held-out combinations. Independent review of actual traces is present. No further generic review tier or exhaustive setting coverage is needed merely to make this preparatory route complete.

The route still needs concrete source, fixture, client, ownership, and recovery decisions through its existing contract tickets. Their absence is expected pending work, not a reason to invent additional initiatives now. No live native graph or tracker state was verified in this review.
