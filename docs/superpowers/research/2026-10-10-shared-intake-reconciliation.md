# Shared intake and cross-cutting effort reconciliation

Praxis already has an accepted owner and contract for direct intake, resumption, and portfolio reconciliation. Wayfinder already supplies destination-first, breadth-first charting once selected. The original concurrency intake received that Wayfinder guidance and nevertheless chose one focused correction ticket. That establishes a missed application in the observed sequence; it does not establish the agent’s underlying cause, universal discovery reliability, or a qualified remedy.

The smallest useful next step is to make work-structure selection an explicit, evaluated branch of Praxis’s accepted shared intake, with minimal pointers from its real callers. Keep Wayfinder optional, preserve existing maps and specialist owners, and measure whether the branch improves outcomes without creating needless planning.

## Bounded correction and adoption proposal

**Proposal:** add an explicit work-structure assessment to the already accepted shared-intake contract. Do not introduce another public planner, persistent portfolio aggregate, or global service.

The assessment should:

1. Preserve the authorized outcome and amendments, separately from the incident that exposed a concern.
2. Scout relevant existing efforts, accepted decisions, maintained source, candidates, current claims, and actual consumers before selecting structure.
3. Determine whether the current work is a small bounded task, a contribution to an adequate existing map, a new coherent map, or a coordination obligation across distinct maps.
4. Return the chosen structure and its reason, named owners, precise unanswered questions, genuine required joins, unresolved fog, and the parent’s remaining delivery/adoption obligations.
5. Repeat the affected assessment on scope growth, changed prerequisites, owner returns, interruption, or resumption with incomplete framing.
6. Preserve missing history, incomplete reads, and consequential uncertainty explicitly. Ask for a value or missing authority only when discovery cannot settle it.

Breadth, owner count, or a ticket’s length should be signals to inspect, not automatic map-creation thresholds. A clear small fix should remain small; an adequate map should be reused.

Compare two placements before implementation:

- A focused branch in `stewarding-agentic-efforts` with one caller pointer: smallest initial exposure and maintenance cost, but potentially duplicated semantics across resumption and portfolio paths.
- One shared assessment in the accepted `plugins/praxis/references/stewardship-contract.md`, loaded by stewardship and portfolio procedures, with minimal descriptions/pointers at their entrypoints: fits the existing accepted architecture and provides one maintained meaning, with added reading cost to measure.

The second is the better fit if both consumers need the same assessment. Neither option requires a new public skill. A helper, hook, or mandatory extra artifact earns inclusion only if measured failures justify it.

Praxis’s steward remains the intake consumer and owner of the parent assessment. `reconciling-work-portfolios` supplies bounded multi-effort observations and proposals. Wayfinder retains planning and tracker mechanics through the optional adapter. Its invocation restrictions must be respected; recognizing a need does not authorize bypassing a user-only entrypoint. Generic intake must remain useful without Wayfinder.

[Adoption sequencing](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/161) should place any accepted correction into compatible delivery increments while preserving predecessor-owned Praxis surfaces. Later delivery must capture the maintained method, review its final revision, establish behavior evidence, publish/project/install through the owners, and verify at least one real caller locating and loading that revision and checking its output. Planning acceptance alone cannot close those obligations.

## Proposed behavior cases

These are future expectations, not observed passes.

| Case | Expected behavior | Failure or negative control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vivid local incident with cross-cutting scope | Preserve both the incident and the wider outcome; scout prior work and owners; distinguish the immediate authorized repair from unresolved program work; select an appropriate graph and retain parent obligations. | Solving the vivid incident, filing one broad checklist, or mentioning Wayfinder is insufficient. |
| Overlapping maps with settled decisions | Derive a scoped view of distinct efforts; preserve accepted decisions, claims, and phase; reuse contributions and name required returns. Add only genuine dependencies. | No duplicate umbrella by default, silent reparenting, blanket blockers, owner takeover, or reopening settled names. |
| Scope growth during investigation | Reassess the destination and work structure when findings introduce a new owner, branch, or delivery obligation; graduate precise work and retain unresolved fog. | Continuing indefinitely inside an obsolete narrow ticket, or expanding authority merely because the scope grew. |
| Incomplete resumption | Recover the original brief, amendments, decisions, grants, consumed results, and outstanding obligations; refresh live state; disclose incomplete history. | Treating a partial handoff as complete intent, reviving superseded work, or inventing missing acceptance. |
| Genuinely small fix | Recognize a clear, authorized, bounded change and use its ordinary owner workflow with proportional checks. | Unnecessary map, portfolio inventory, policy-construction ceremony, or extra planning exchange. |
| Adequate existing map | Verify that its destination, owners, accepted decisions, frontier, and adoption accounting cover the request; reuse it and update only the newly precise contribution when authorized. | Creating another map, copying decisions, repeating completed research, or confusing planning completion with delivery. |

Positive discovery must test whether an ordinary brief selects the relevant intake entrypoint before choosing structure. Negative discovery must test near misses that should use the ordinary small-work path. Application tests then check actual reads, decisions, authorized writes, resulting graph semantics, and parent-obligation accounting. A correctly invoked no-op is not evidence for negative discovery.

Use original-state incident reproductions where exposure is recoverable; report an unmet baseline if the historical failure does not reproduce. Keep held-out incidents and materially different workflows so the candidate cannot succeed by recognizing concurrency-specific vocabulary.

Compare ordinary applicable practice with the assessment branch under matched quality and authority. Record preserved intent, ownership accuracy, frontier/fog accuracy, required joins, duplicate work, inappropriate planning, unsupported holds, and fulfilled parent obligations. Count source reads, setup, worker/controller turns, tokens, tools, retries, reconstruction, elapsed time, review, and human rescue across successful, failed, held, and unresolved attempts. No ticket count, round count, or unmeasured reduction in steering proves improvement.
