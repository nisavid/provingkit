# A real attribution boundary, with interpretation production still unproved

The research-publication workflow provides an explicitly linked report that a summary can be checked against. It does not provide a cheap, complete producer of competing interpretations. This investigation therefore identifies a narrower possible use for Jev—checking whether a summary faithfully represents its named source—without qualifying the proposed ambiguity-handling mechanism or selecting that new purpose.

The [real-input investigation](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/678) follows the [accepted preparation choice](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/608#issuecomment-6277942817). Its findings apply to the examined research-authoring artifacts and existing producers, not to every potential workload.

## What the real artifacts supply

The concrete consumer is the coordinator writing the [catalog-frontier publication](../jev-axi-catalog-frontier-2026-10-19/README.md). Its description of the opportunity report says that the report “proposes publication reconciliation first,” explicitly links that report, and separately explains why its ranking cannot be used as the current recommendation.

The existing [report identities](../jev-axi-catalog-frontier-2026-10-19/report-identities.json) identify the design report, opportunity report, and later synthesis by path, byte count, and digest. Code can check these identities and the declared output bounds. These checks need no semantic classifier.

The explicit link selects one report. It does not settle the meaning of every qualification in the summary, and it does not prove an exhaustive set of readings for phrases such as “first” or “the current recommendation.” Listing the three reports as candidates would introduce irrelevant alternatives to an explicitly attributed sentence. Conversely, treating their identities as the complete semantic domain would hide scope and amendment questions.

| Proposed input boundary | What is established | What remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report identity and output length | Existing files and recorded identities support deterministic comparison. | No residual Jev judgment for these checks. |
| Summary explicitly attributed to one report | The published assertion, linked report, and surrounding qualifications exist. | Semantic faithfulness, timely draft delivery, recurring costs, and useful consumer effect. |
| General conclusion across several reports | Sources and later corrections are preserved. | Complete valid interpretations, supported selection rules, interpretation costs, and semantic adequacy. |

## Producer feasibility and costs

The [claim producer](../jev-axi-comparison-producers-2026-10-07/claim_producer.py) takes a caller-selected assertion, draft coordinates, and one or two sources pinned to commits and hashes. It checks those identities, preserves context, and tests literal quotation matching. It discovers neither citations nor interpretations. On its successful source path, three Git subprocesses per artifact give six to nine calls for one draft and one or two sources. This is a source-derived operation count, not a measured workflow cost.

A working draft is outside that committed-input interface. The [completed observation](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/585#issuecomment-6124915281) already demonstrated the timing gap: draft capture finished before the reported review-launch acknowledgment, but assertion selection and source preparation finished afterward. Repeating the same collection would not establish a new early delivery mechanism.

For the narrower attribution purpose, a producer would need the unchanged draft sentence and enclosing qualifications, its explicit source selection, complete source text, and revision/amendment context. It must not supply a precomputed verdict about whether the summary is faithful. Existing files establish availability by publication; they do not establish that this complete package reached the consumer before useful checking work was finished.

For the original multiple-interpretation design, each valid joint binding needs a judgment, and deterministic code combines the relations. Independent phrases do not justify a Cartesian product: that may invent invalid readings. No supplied producer establishes a complete set. Missing preparation is an input failure, not a correct abstention.

Recurring acquisition, selection, interpretation production, repeated tokens, judgments, fallback, corrections, operator inspection, maintenance, and terminal work remain unmeasured. Unknown adaptation cost prevents claiming an economical path; it does not prove that further preparation is impossible. Keep actual charges, API-equivalent value, model capacity, latency, and operator effort separate.

## Decision consequences

The proposed seven-case ambiguity test is not ready to establish a real integration opportunity. It could test judgments with authored bindings, but this investigation has not supplied the real complete-input path it was meant to precede.

The narrower attribution purpose is plausible because the original source selector already exists and preparing it need not decide semantic faithfulness. It is not evidence that Jev improves the resulting handback, saves work, or beats ordinary adjacent source reading. It also does not rescue the failed general ambiguity designs. Any future comparison retains omission, deterministic preparation, ordinary reasoning, Jev, Decisions, Luna 6 Low/Medium/High, and Sol 6.1 Low as applicable; earlier scores select none.

The next scope choice has three distinct branches:

1. Prepare explicit-source attribution as a deliberately narrower purpose. Establish supported working-draft delivery and preparation cost before proposing another semantic or workflow experiment.
2. Continue looking for a real domain that naturally supplies complete joint bindings, while preserving this examined workflow's negative result for that broader mechanism.
3. Set this claim-checking work aside and prioritize the existing compatibility startup investigation. Its equipment already exists; it does not depend on completing this branch.

If the first branch is selected, input/delivery preparation and ordinary-work/accounting preparation can proceed independently on the same attributed assertion, then join before contract review. Do not omit required review or declare replacement savings unless a specific review step is actually removed. A plausible addition or operator benefit need not wait for an observed ordinary failure, but it must still demonstrate value without added false intervention or reduced quality. New collection, implementation, and experimental execution remain separate scope decisions.

## Research evidence and closure

[Workload first pass](workload-first-pass.md) and [accounting first pass](accounting-first-pass.md) used the same 48-file, 309,273-byte frozen packet. Both verified its identities and returned before receiving any sibling output. Their conclusions above were joined by the coordinator against the source. Each return met its word/byte bounds and was recorded usable as research input; neither is an integration qualification.

Both native workers completed, both live controllers recorded their results and exited zero, and both admission loops have verified generation-bound finished-history records. Fresh resource checks admitted each under the already-enabled bounded-analysis policy and its declared complete 4 GiB local envelope. The native route establishes requested Sol 6.1 High selection and observed completion, not product-attested model identity.

No new observation, provider assessment, model comparison, producer implementation, live change, or upstream submission occurred. All earlier failed and expired attempts, comparison results, and historical controller-closure uncertainty remain preserved. The raw reports' links use repository-root-relative paths; their byte identities are retained in [the manifest](report-identities.json).
