# Completion evidence: two observation proposals

Completion-claim support is worth investigating as evidence reconciliation and reduced operator verification effort. The retained sample establishes neither a missed current obligation nor removable agent review work; successful boundaries still provide concrete material for investigating avoidable reconstruction.

## Evidence and scope

I used `research` within the frozen read-only contract. All ten input hashes matched before and after inspection; canonical plan and UTF-8 request hashes matched. The declared dispatch binding matches the supplied binding; its serialization was unavailable for independent recomputation. No sibling outputs, private records, network, tests, hooks, models, application execution, writes, or subdelegation were used. The coordinator saves this artifact.

The [accepted purposes][acceptance] distinguish unmet requirements from sufficient verification, obsolete requirements, pauses, bounded handoffs, and partial context. The [exploratory portfolio][portfolio] additionally permits measured operator effort reduction without unnecessary questions or task-quality loss. Its broader scope supersedes the earlier search recommendation, not the audit's evidentiary limits.

**Source-derived mechanics:** the [Stop trace][trace] reports first/latest prompt extraction, bounded job/diff/tool-output context, no extracted final response or structured boundary meaning, and a baseline produced after the first PostToolUse event. These mechanics cannot establish current intent, complete change coverage, or verification sufficiency by themselves.

**Retained native evidence:** the [audit][audit] describes eight historical passive boundaries with assessments omitted and no steering. Twenty-two recovered JSON records matched historical identities; no complete hook record or selected original native stdout was recovered. Delivered requests, selected completion messages, constructed observations, artifact checks, and historical inspections survive through distinct provenance classes. Boundary digests identify lost originals; they do not make projections complete originals.

The [ordinary audit][ordinary] provides useful successful contrasts: existing parser tests remained unchanged and passed; report verification used commands/artifacts without a required new test file; the final Claude sorting projection omitted the intermediate JSON amendment although diff/output retained JSON. Projection loss establishes missing input, not a wrong verdict.

The [representative audit][representative] retains initial CSV and amended JSON boundaries with ten and sixteen reported passing tests. Earlier failures coexist with later passes; the second diff and both output tails are incomplete. Subsequent controller-censored sorting is not a false completion claim at either earlier boundary. Process exit, a passing check, report-artifact acceptance, and whole-task correctness remain different claims.

## Proposal 1: bounded claim-to-evidence reconciliation

**Consumer and benefit:** the completing native agent decides whether its proposed completion statement has support. Predicted benefit is a useful correction or reduced observed reconciliation work; absent a measured removable step, this is an addition.

**Inputs and producers:** select ordered operator requests/amendments, applicable repository requirements, proposed final claims, task-owned candidate identity, boundary kind, and check/result identities from existing native records. The operator originates intent; the agent must retrieve applicable requirements and compose claims; harness/tool records supply receipts. Deterministic collection can preserve identities, ordering, coverage, and omissions. Any agent-produced requirement interpretation and claim/evidence pairing is recurring paid work, not free structured intent. Neither complete hook-time context nor an observed producer for the complete pairing currently survives.

**Comparators and judgment:** compare ordinary reconciliation, omission of added assistance, and deterministic presence/freshness checks. A genuine bounded Jev question could assess whether specified evidence supports one supplied requirement/claim pair, with an insufficient-input outcome. Jev cannot recover omitted intent, generate the pairing for free, or verify application behavior.

**Contrasts:** unchanged existing parser tests versus a genuinely missing required check; successful JSON amendment versus obsolete CSV requirements; later passing checks versus unresolved failures; completion versus explicit pause/handoff; complete versus truncated coverage. Changed candidate identity invalidates affected verification deterministically before semantic judgment.

**Defeat and cheapest observation:** first annotate the eight retained boundaries against supplied requests without inventing missing records. Determine which ambiguities remain after presence/freshness checks, marking historical findings separately from receipts. Synthetic missing-check pairs can test question feasibility only. If preparation merely repeats ordinary review, useful evidence is inaccessible, or no actionable semantic ambiguity remains, defer Jev. Native correction or removed work requires a separately accepted ordinary-task observation.

## Proposal 2: inspectable completion evidence for the operator

**Consumer and benefit:** the operator decides whether to accept a completion claim or inspect a specific gap. Predicted benefit is less evidence-search/reconstruction effort on successful work, with unchanged trust and task quality.

**Inputs and producers:** reuse Proposal 1's bounded records and expose source-linked claims, current candidate/check identities, superseded checks, and explicit unknowns alongside the ordinary final response. The agent selects and writes; native collection supplies references; retention preserves bytes. Selection, refresh, retention, and operator inspection are costs. This proposes a bounded presentation of existing evidence, not a new requirements ledger or completion certificate.

**Comparators and judgment:** compare the ordinary final response, deterministic linked receipts with identity/freshness checks, and omission. Jev is optional: its only candidate contribution is semantic relevance assessment of supplied pairs. Measure that increment separately from better collection/presentation.

**Contrasts:** successful CSV/JSON reports requiring reconstruction versus readily inspectable receipts; artifact acceptance versus generator behavior; stale or partial evidence versus current supported checks; pauses/handoffs versus completion. Evidence must not make incomplete records appear authoritative.

**Defeat and cheapest observation:** after separate acceptance, observe one ordinary successful task and the operator's actual verification actions/time, then compare bounded evidence presentations using the same preserved candidate. Count preparation and refresh, questions, misleading confidence, inspection/rework, and final quality. If equivalent decisions require equal or greater total effort, or trust/quality declines, the proposal fails its stated benefit.

## Shared prerequisites and next decision

Continuity can reuse candidate identities, current amendments, unresolved obligations, and retention; discovery may supply verification capabilities at use time. These are shared interfaces, not findings from sibling lanes. Charge shared collection once; count each consumer's marginal work separately. Passive capture intervals are research measurements, not production latency. Operator effort, recurring costs, billing, capacity, and replacement savings remain unknown.

Both proposals are concrete preparation leads with named missing observations. Neither qualifies a behavior or selects an event, assignment, experiment, or authority. Any next contract must follow the [maintained comparison requirements][method] and retain complete evidence durably.
