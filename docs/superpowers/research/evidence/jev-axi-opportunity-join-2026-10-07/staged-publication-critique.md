# Put semantic claim checking beside the evidence views

The three reports identify plausible improvements, but their agreement does not establish three useful interventions. All favor organizing existing evidence, and much of that is already ordinary good practice. I would elevate **claim-to-source checking during research authoring** to the next scope discussion, alongside one bounded publication-coverage sketch. Completion links and optional-review preparation should remain ordinary practices unless a distinct integration benefit emerges.

## Where the recommendations need correction

**My publication recommendation overstated what deterministic preparation can expose.** Comparing outgoing paths with recorded digests can identify a mismatch or absence from a supplied record. It cannot generally identify every “required check whose evidence is stale or inapplicable.” Those conclusions need authoritative obligations and dependency coverage. The implementation should initially report mechanical differences; ordinary interpretation owns their significance.

The nine-outgoing-files/seven-reviewed-content-files example is a useful adversarial case for that implementation, but not yet a compelling benefit case. The original publication report already explains the difference. Reformatting that explanation may accomplish nothing. Its value depends on whether the comparison exposes a new difference across commits or materially reduces reconstruction.

**The completion proposal correctly treats ordinary direct links as its strongest rival.** That should remain an explicit baseline, not become a weaker report with inconvenient navigation. Its proposed view is an integration improvement only if preservation, result access, or candidate association improves. A differently formatted summary is insufficient.

**The allocation proposal names a real decision, but much of its preparation may already be the decision.** Selecting the relevant question, passages, review scope, and missing premise can settle whether another reviewer would help. Routing the resulting packet afterward may merely classify the coordinator’s conclusion. Existing questions bound the producer better than newly generated questions, but do not make evidence selection free. Its strongest setting is scattered questions or a genuine handoff, not a coordinator’s freshly authored synthesis.

These are scope corrections, not findings that the ideas lack value.

## Why semantic checking deserves equal consideration

Hashes and links establish identity and access. They cannot determine whether an author has turned source preparation into qualification, a witness report into independently verified behavior, or a later successful outcome into an observed intervention benefit.

That residual question appears across all three reports. It can affect a published research conclusion even when every reference resolves and every recorded digest matches. Its potential benefit is **earlier correction of unsupported inference**, including reduced downstream revision. It need not remove a required review to be worth investigating.

My first report ranked it second largely because its producer looked more expensive. That is not enough to establish priority. Producer cost belongs in the comparison; missing measurements do not defeat a useful hypothesis.

A narrow candidate is:

> During research authoring, assess whether a selected material claim preserves the evidence boundary of its cited passage.

For example, “The later push succeeded” and “The observation validated successful publication” require different judgments against the same publication result. The source places the successful push outside the observation. This is an authored contrast, not a recovered author error. [Publication result](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/blob/ca2c510c304cb7fd3216a1f2b0ac02645dceea12/docs/superpowers/research/evidence/jev-axi-publication-observation-2026-10-05/results.md).

## The producer must preserve the judgment

The author produces the draft claim and citation during ordinary writing. Retrieval supplies the cited passage, surrounding qualifications, and source identity. Jev could assess support, contradiction, or insufficient evidence; it cannot discover all material claims, supply missing sources, or write the correction.

Do not require the producer to rewrite every pair into a fully adjudicated explanation before testing the classifier. That would perform the proposed judgment upstream. Equally, do not omit context simply to make preparation cheap. Keep evaluator labels separate from candidate inputs.

The TypeSafe cookbook supplies a concrete decomposition—quote matching followed by semantic relationship classification—but its small, partly planted example establishes feasibility only. It does not settle production context selection or local benefit. [Citation cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check.md).

## Most informative next scopes

Prepare two independent, reviewable proposals:

1. **Semantic claim support.** Use selected public claims with their original citations and surrounding context. Specify ordinary author reading, deterministic retrieval/quote checks, and the same preparation plus a Jev challenger. Include accurate limitations, unsupported inference, genuinely contradictory evidence, and multi-source claims that require abstention or ordinary review. Compare the complete authoring and correction workflow; retain required final reviews. Authored contrasts can test feasibility, while a later accepted native comparison must establish useful effect.
2. **Publication coverage.** Sketch the outgoing-change comparison directly from existing public records. Demonstrate what it reveals beyond the current report, including changed bytes, an added outgoing document, and supporting records outside the content manifest. If interpretation requires a comprehensive new ledger, narrow or reject this implementation.

Neither preparation must await the other. Shared source retrieval can be reused, with costs counted once and benefits attributed separately.

Bring Ivan the choice between **earlier semantic correction**, **easier publication reconciliation**, and **ordinary practice without new assistance**. Claim checking deserves priority if the bounded producer preserves a meaningful judgment and the resulting correction could matter. Publication preparation deserves priority if it reveals consequential differences with little reconstruction. Neither needs a historical failure to merit that decision.
