# Compare semantic support and publication coverage

Two distinct opportunities are ready for scope selection: checking whether a research claim overstates its cited evidence, and making the relationship between outgoing changes and existing review records easier to inspect. I recommend considering their preparation in parallel. Neither has been selected for an experiment, assigned to Jev, or qualified for ordinary use.

The three independent research lanes initially favored organizing existing evidence. Cross-informed critique changed that ranking: useful navigation and record comparison cannot answer whether an inference is supported. The semantic opportunity deserves consideration now, without waiting for an evidence-view experiment or an observed authoring failure.

## The decisions each candidate could improve

| Candidate | Consumer and decision | Proposed assistance | Serious comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claim support | Research author deciding whether a material assertion needs rereading, qualification, or correction | Relate a supplied claim to its cited passage and relevant qualifications before required final review | Ordinary adjacent reading; deterministic retrieval and quote checks; identical preparation plus a Jev judgment |
| Publication coverage | Publishing agent reconciling the proposed outgoing range with existing review records | Show matching recorded bytes, changed bytes, and paths absent from the supplied record | Ordinary publication preparation and its existing evidence presentation; a deterministic difference view; no added view |

For claim support, the proposed Jev output concerns the supplied evidence. It cannot certify global truth, find every material claim, retrieve missing evidence, or write the correction. An uncertain result does not automatically require a question to Ivan. The author retains interpretation and correction, and required reviews remain required.

Publication coverage initially reports mechanical differences. Calling an absent record a missing required review, or a matching digest a current complete review, requires additional facts about obligations, scope, and dependencies. Those facts cannot be assumed or silently supplied by the adapter.

The subsequent [completion critique](completion-discovery-critique.md), [publication critique](staged-publication-critique.md), and [supervision critique](supervision-allocation-critique.md) correct three weaknesses in that first ranking:

1. Evidence organization may duplicate ordinary compliant work. Completion links and README discovery remain baselines; optional-review preparation is a possible consumer, not a justified new infrastructure project.
2. Cheap preparation does not by itself make a candidate preferable. More expensive context production can be worthwhile if the complete workflow yields sufficient benefit.
3. An addition can earn its role by improving outcomes or reducing later rework. Removing an existing review step is one route to value, not a universal prerequisite. Required reviews remain intact.

These corrections retain the governing assignment criteria. Context preparation is part of the candidate's cost, and benefit from better access does not establish Jev's incremental value.

## Feasibility precedents and their limits

TypeSafe's [citation-checking cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check.md) combines deterministic quote matching with a semantic relationship judgment. Its eight examples include four edited failures and replay cached results. This establishes a concrete implementation precedent, not local reliability, savings, a confidence threshold, or a complete production context-selection method. An unmatched quote should remain a mismatch rather than inheriting the example's stronger fabrication label.

TypeSafe's [skill-suggestion cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/skill_suggestion.md) reports both corrected and spoiled selections in a different, synthetic routing task. Its result is a reason to include matched legitimate cases and false steering in qualification; it is not evidence that claim checking or review allocation works here.

## Proposed next preparation and join

The human scope decision lives in [Choose the next catalog comparison from the joined opportunities](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/448). This research recommends two independently useful preparation scopes:

- **Semantic support:** specify a bounded public claim and its original citation/context, the author decision that assistance could change, and the ordinary, deterministic, and Jev-assisted alternatives. Include accurate limits, unsupported extensions, contradictory evidence, missing context, and multi-source cases whose answer may require ordinary review. Keep evaluator answers separate from candidate inputs.
- **Publication coverage:** sketch the ordinary presentation and proposed difference view for the retained commit candidate, authored changed content, an authored extra outgoing document, and supporting records absent from the content manifest. Identify every conclusion that still requires interpretation.

If selected, evidence relevance and producer feasibility can be investigated concurrently for each scope. The semantic producer must not adjudicate the pair before handing it to the proposed judge. The publication producer must not require a new comprehensive semantic ledger while presenting itself as cheap record comparison. Neither scope needs a new history collector merely to prepare its design.

Join the exact preparation revisions before proposing a concrete experiment. Shared retrieval may be reused, but count its production once and retain standalone and marginal costs. Keep author, coordinator, reviewer, and operator outcomes separate. Include preparation, all model attempts, flags, corrections, repeated checking, failures, maintenance, latency, and money; comparative totals remain unknown.

Ordinary work can win either comparison. A view that only repeats an already usable report should be omitted. A semantic check can win as an addition if it yields useful correction at acceptable complete cost, even when it removes no required review. Authored examples can establish a tractable test, not native benefit or general reliability.
