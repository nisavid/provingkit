# Elevate semantic claim checking alongside evidence access

The three reports identify plausible integration improvements, but their shared preference for organizing evidence risks repackaging ordinary good practice. I would elevate the publication lane’s semantic claim-checking rival for independent preparation now. It need not wait for an evidence-view experiment or an observed authoring failure.

## What should change in the join

**Adequate existing practice:** an ordinary report already links a claim to understandable evidence; a coordinator already knows which question needs review; publication preparation already reconciles its outgoing content. Reformatting these facts adds no distinct capability.

**Specific integration improvement:** retain a result reference when verification happens, expose recorded coverage when Ivan opens the claim, or compare an outgoing range with an existing review manifest. These proposals change access or reconciliation. They deserve investigation where ordinary presentation leaves actual navigation or comparison work; no classifier is needed for those mechanics.

**Residual semantic judgment:** determine whether a supplied passage supports the claim drawn from it. Matching hashes, accessible links, and current review identities cannot answer that question. This is a distinct plausible Jev assignment.

The [completion report](completion-discovery-first-pass.md) properly retains equally good ordinary links as a rival. The [publication report](staged-publication-first-pass.md) supplies a concrete nine-outgoing-files versus seven-reviewed-content-files case, which gives its proposed comparison more substance than a generic evidence dashboard. My [allocation report](supervision-allocation-first-pass.md) has the weakest independent implementation case: its author already supplies questions and evidence, and the coordinator still makes every consequential interpretation. Keep that proposal as a possible consumer of better evidence preparation, rather than a third infrastructure project.

Two formulations need correction. My requirement that Jev selection work be “substantial enough to plausibly replace” is too narrow: a useful addition can earn its cost without replacing a step. Likewise, producing claim–passage pairs may be expensive without defeating semantic checking. The question is whether total preparation and checking buy sufficient improvement—not whether the producer is cheap.

## Why claim checking deserves its own scope

Use the research author as consumer, immediately before required final review. The decision is whether a selected source-dependent sentence needs rereading, qualification, or correction. Required reviews still assess the final revision.

A sharper authored contrast than an obviously false publication claim is:

> “The saved pause record preserved the chosen review method, so it avoided reconstruction work.”

The [resumption result](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/blob/ca2c510c304cb7fd3216a1f2b0ac02645dceea12/docs/superpowers/research/evidence/jev-axi-resumption-study-2026-10-06/results.md) supports retrieval and later use of that choice. It does not establish avoided reconstruction or sole causation. A correct citation and matching digest would survive this unsupported extension.

The author already supplies the sentence and citation. Retrieval supplies the cited section with its relevant qualifications. Selecting sufficient context, splitting compound claims, and checking whether other sources matter are additional producer costs; they must remain visible. Start with source-dependent claims whose evidence can be bounded, rather than whole-task completion or arbitrary multi-source arguments.

Jev’s proposed output is a relationship to the **supplied evidence**, with insufficient-context handling. It is not certification that the claim is globally true or false. The author inspects a flagged pair and owns correction; uncertainty does not automatically generate a question for Ivan.

A useful addition might catch an unsupported inference before it reaches the required reviewer or reduce a correction round. These are hypotheses, not observed savings. Ordinary reading of the identical claim and passage is the serious rival.

## Source check and limits

I independently checked TypeSafe’s [citation cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check.md). The publication report accurately describes its deterministic quote matching, semantic section comparison, eight examples, four edited failures, and cached results. The cookbook explicitly acknowledges that normalized exact matching labels truncated or lightly reworded quotations “fabricated.” Retain the report’s proposed neutral mismatch label. Its source-specific parser, three semantic outcomes, and automatic-confidence rule also cannot be imported as a complete local policy.

This establishes an implementation precedent, not review effectiveness. The earlier skill-suggestion findings remain a caution: advisory classifications can spoil previously correct decisions. Correctly limited claims, missing context, and misleading partial support must therefore be critical contrasts.
