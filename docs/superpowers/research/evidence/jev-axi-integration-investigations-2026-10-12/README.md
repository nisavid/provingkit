# Find where an early claim check could improve the work

I recommend one prospective observation of a real research draft before final
review, following a material assertion through source selection, reuse, review,
and publication preparation. The question is concrete: **can an early System
One check receive adequate evidence cheaply enough, and soon enough, to reduce
support-checking effort or prevent an overstatement from causing later rework?**

This is the coordinator's recommendation for
[Choose the next System One integration hypothesis and discriminating test](https://github.com/nisavid/provingkit/issues/558).
It is not an accepted observation contract or an integration assignment. The two
authorized investigations ran concurrently and are complete. Their independent
first reports are retained in [workflow investigation](workflow-investigation.md)
and [error investigation](errors-investigation.md); their bindings and intake
checks are in [the research record](research-record.json).

## What the investigations establish

The workflow investigation traced the producer code and worked examples. The
claim producer verifies and packages a claim and its supplied sources, but
someone must first select the assertion, identify one or two sources, and supply
an immutable draft. Its current reader requires a Git commit. The comparison
therefore began after selection work whose ordinary cost and timing were not
measured. A result that needs a special commit or substantial source rereading
may arrive too late to save that work.

The error investigation reconstructed the actual claim requests. All six routes
received byte-identical evidence text for each case, although API representation
and instruction placement differed. Jev accepted three publication-scope
overstatements. For example, it accepted “The retained publication involved nine
outgoing files” even though the supplied source explicitly distinguished a
nine-file commit candidate from an unavailable actual pushed range. Missing that
distinction from the request cannot by itself explain the error. Question design,
source roles, salience, route behavior, and model limitations remain competing
explanations.

The actual question already instructed candidates not to treat a derivative
assertion as independent evidence or an unestablished event as a contradicted
event. Repeating those instructions is not a distinct correction. Decisions'
five `unresolved` results were completed classifications, not missing deliveries;
in the accepted categories, that answer is not a generic low-confidence fallback.

The publication-coverage failures also differ. Some notes invented missing-review
obligations; others retrieved material but omitted its interpretation or the
separate review association. One note introduced an unsupported publication
claim. The existing deterministic relationship summary did not resolve them.
Direct changed-text delivery and a more explicit completion structure are
plausible improvements, but would test ordinary evidence handling rather than
establish a Jev assignment.

These findings leave the published comparison outcomes unchanged: Jev matched
seven of ten supplied claim labels, Decisions three, all three Luna efforts ten,
and Sol Low nine. No coverage condition passed both critical cases. Those are
bounded observations, not general model rankings. See the original
[claim results](../jev-axi-ordinary-results-2026-10-11/claim-results.md) and
[coverage results](../jev-axi-ordinary-results-2026-10-11/coverage-results.md).

## How the two recommendations join

The workflow report recommends observing input readiness and remaining work.
The error report recommends a fresh coverage-delivery comparison, conditional
on finding useful reconciliation work. The workflow observation answers that
condition while also serving the more direct System One opportunity: an early
typed claim judgment. I would lead with it and retain the coverage comparison
as a conditional next step.

I qualify one inference in both first reports. **An author having answered a
question does not make independent checking useless.** The retained nine-file
overstatement was caught by ordinary final review. An earlier independent check
could still help if it catches an error before reuse and reduces later correction
work. The two benefit claims require different evidence:

- **Replace reasoning work:** show which support-checking work would actually
  disappear. Selecting the evidence after doing that same work cannot establish
  this benefit.
- **Add useful error checking:** show earlier correction, less propagation or
  rework, and preserved final quality after counting preparation, consumption,
  false alarms, and required review. No separate reasoning step need disappear.

The first reports remain unchanged so this qualification is visible. The join
does not adopt their blanket inference that prior resolution alone defeats an
added semantic check. Nor does one task without a useful opportunity establish
that the purpose is generally unhelpful.

There is no material factual disagreement requiring a steering panel at this
point. The missing fact is an observable workflow boundary. A panel cannot
establish when inputs arrive or what their preparation costs.

## The next test I recommend

Use one ordinary research-document task with a bounded section selected
prospectively. Follow its first material evidence-based assertion and ordinary
citations. Do not insert a wrong claim or select only a known defect. Preserve
the existing one-assertion, one-or-two-source input scope.

At an existing draft checkpoint before final review, retain:

1. The draft assertion, cited sources, and their identities, including when each
   became available. If the current commit-based reader cannot consume this
   checkpoint, record that specific limitation; do not create an extra commit
   and count it as ordinary work.
2. The work needed to select and prepare those inputs, separated from the
   author's ordinary citation and source work. Prefer existing task-owned
   artifacts and explicit actions; this proposal does not require another broad
   history collector.
3. Observable support explanations, subsequent source reads, claim edits,
   downstream reuse, final-review findings, and resulting revisions. Do not
   infer the author's internal reasoning or treat a statement of confidence as
   proof of correct support.
4. At the same task's ordinary publication preparation, the chosen comparison
   and review records, manual reconciliation, changed-text inspection, and final
   explanation. This identifies whether deterministic delivery has useful work
   to remove without creating a separate artificial workload.

This first observation delivers no model hint. It can establish an available
intervention point and its costs; it cannot establish the counterfactual effect
of an intervention. Keep observation overhead separate from ordinary work, count
shared collection once, and leave unmeasured effort unknown.

| Observation | Decision it would inform |
| --- | --- |
| Adequate inputs exist before substantial support checking or before reuse of a claim later corrected in review | Prepare a focused early claim-check comparison at that point. Distinguish reasoning replacement from added error checking. |
| Inputs become available only after the proposed benefit could occur, or special preparation repeats the work it was intended to save | Do not advance this placement as a work-saving replacement. An independent-check proposal still needs its own concrete benefit hypothesis. |
| Repeated publication navigation remains after record selection | Prepare the changed-text delivery comparison, retaining ordinary reconciliation as its baseline. |
| Ordinary reporting already supplies the useful result with no identified remaining benefit | Retain ordinary handling for this observed scope. Further work needs a different, specific hypothesis. |
| Inputs, events, or costs cannot be established | Return the precise missing fact; do not turn missing evidence into success or initiate an automatic replacement observation. |

The concrete contract still needs a suitable workload, verified current artifact
sources, collection bounds, and execution acceptance. One observation is not a
frequency estimate or a new universal gate on exploratory research.

## Where Jev and the other models fit

The candidate integration is a narrow relation check, delivered to the author
before reuse or required review. Code establishes source identities and literal
relationships. Jev would answer a semantic question about whether the supplied
evidence warrants the assertion. The author consumes the result, consults the
evidence where needed, and owns any revision. This proposes neither an automatic
veto nor removal of required review.

If the observation supports that placement, the existing failed question must
be improved and checked on fresh cases before native intervention. A concrete
design alternative is to separate two questions: does a source establish an
incompatible fact, and does it establish the complete assertion at its stated
scope? Missing referents and genuine source conflicts need explicit handling
before mapping back to the same four accepted relations. This is a proposed
design, not a specified or qualified classifier.

Compare that design with the current four-way question while holding evidence
constant first. Include accurate bounded claims, overstatements, explicit
contradictions, and genuinely conflicting-source controls. Use fresh material;
the retained failures are development evidence. Context-role or salience changes
would be a separate factor rather than silently changing question and context
together.

Keep Jev, Decisions, Luna 6 Low/Medium/High, and Sol 6.1 Low in the relevant
comparison. A later native consumer comparison must retain ordinary authoring,
deterministic preparation, and omission, and measure the final authored work.
Passing fresh relation cases would justify that preparation, not establish
native benefit or economic superiority.

No confidence threshold is selected. Jev's three wrong supported answers had
higher confidence than its correct unsupported-extension answer on another
case. TypeSafe also documents Choice confidence as a transformation of option
probabilities, rather than an independent correctness measure. Its citation
cookbook supplies a useful architectural example, not a threshold validated for
this workflow. See [Choice confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md)
and [the citation-checking example](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check.md),
retrieved October 12, 2026. If a score will govern action, its rule needs separate
purpose-specific evaluation, including abstention and fallback costs.

## Conditional publication-delivery test

The error report specifies a useful later diagnostic: on fresh unchanged and
changed-text cases, compare the current summary, the same evidence with explicit
completion fields, and those fields with the complete changed texts supplied
directly. Exact counts and digest relationships should be rendered by code rather
than regenerated by the model. This separates final-note completion from evidence
navigation while retaining false-intervention controls.

That diagnostic has a place if publication work shows the relevant effort or
errors. It does not assign hashing, counting, or full publication-note generation
to Jev. Its proposed twelve cells and two ordinary profiles are an investigator's
design suggestion, not a selected experiment or restriction on the broader model
comparisons.
