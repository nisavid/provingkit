The next useful test is a prospective observation of one ordinary research-authoring task through its publication-preparation checkpoint. Record when the existing producers’ required inputs become available, what the author or publisher has already decided by then, and what reconciliation work remains. This can distinguish a useful early claim judgment from redundant checking and distinguish useful deterministic delivery from a table that merely repeats an existing explanation.

The retained comparisons establish supplied-case behavior. They do not establish that an added assessment improves native authoring or publication work. The strongest new finding from the source trace is that **both producers begin after consequential selection work that the comparisons did not measure**. The claim producer requires an already designated assertion, immutable draft coordinates, and selected pinned sources. The coverage producer requires an explicit direct-parent commit comparison and caller-selected verification and review records. Neither discovers those inputs from ordinary work. Their downstream usefulness therefore depends on a timing and acquisition boundary that remains unobserved. ## New findings from the workflow trace

### Claim preparation does not find the claim or resolve its citations

The claim producer accepts a structured case containing:

- An assertion.
- A draft commit, path, digest, and enclosing/assertion line coordinates.
- One or two source commit/path/digest entries.
- An optional direct quotation.

It verifies those identities, checks that the designated assertion appears on the specified draft line, preserves complete source text, and performs literal quotation matching. It does not parse the draft’s Markdown citations to discover the sources or determine whether the supplied source list exhausts the assertion’s basis. Those decisions remain with the caller. [Claim producer, `validate` and `prepare`, lines 42–75 and 104–148][claim-producer].

This matters at the proposed early intervention point. The caller might supply the assertion and citations as ordinary writing artifacts before resolving their evidentiary scope. Alternatively, selecting the precise assertion and pinned sources for a special check might require the same rereading that resolves the question. The retained comparison supplied this work in advance and cannot distinguish those possibilities. The preparation explicitly recognizes the redundancy risk, but the implementation now makes its required selection inputs concrete. [Claim preparation, lines 26–39][claim-preparation]; [claim execution contract, lines 13–15 and 49][claim-execution].

A second timing constraint is the immutable draft. `read_blob` requires a commit object; this producer cannot inspect an unsaved or merely working-tree draft. An early integration must therefore find an existing pre-review immutable draft checkpoint or prepare a separately reviewed snapshot input path. Creating extra checkpoints solely to feed the check would be additional work to measure. The supplied evidence does not show which route ordinary authoring provides. [Claim producer, `read_blob`, lines 21–39][claim-producer].

There is also a delivery distinction: the producer stores `quotation_status` in JSON, but the rendered Markdown does not display it. The claim execution contract correctly warns against describing metadata-only quotation matching as delivered information. A future deterministic arm must say which artifact the author actually receives. [Claim producer, lines 131–145][claim-producer]; [claim execution contract, line 15][claim-execution].

It could remove retrieval and cross-referencing work, but the ordinary arm must remain free to produce an equally useful current report. The earlier preparation already protects that strong baseline. The missing fact is whether ordinary publication preparation actually repeats this reconciliation, and whether the proposed producer can supply the current range and relevant records without expensive semantic record selection. The producer accepts caller-selected records and requires a direct-parent commit comparison. It does not establish actual outgoing refs or choose the applicable review. ## Competing integration hypotheses

### Hypothesis A: an early typed claim relation changes later author work

**Consumer and output.** Give the author the designated claim, complete pinned sources, acquisition statuses, and a typed `claim_relation`: `supported`, `contradicted`, `unsupported_extension`, or `unresolved`. A Jev service arm is defensible for this finite output shape. Decisions and ordinary models answering the same question remain controls. The existing tested Jev candidate has unresolved feasibility failures; this proposal does not qualify it. [Claim execution contract, lines 17–36][claim-execution]; [claim results, lines 7–35][claim-results].

**Timing.** Deliver the result after ordinary claim/citation production and before the claim is reused in synthesis or a review package. A judgment arriving after the author resolves the support question is an unused or redundant result.

**Use.** An unsupported-extension or contradiction hint directs the author back to the already supplied evidence. The author determines the edit; the typed answer generates no corrected sentence. Supported claims can receive correct non-action.

**Uncertainty and failure.** Non-ready acquisition produces no semantic call. An unresolved relation preserves the limitation and ordinary interpretation. Invalid output or service failure falls back to ordinary authoring. No returned probability governs an intervention without a separately evaluated decision rule. [Claim preparation, lines 65–96][claim-preparation]; [comparison contract, lines 17–36][contract].

**Work added and potentially removed.** This adds selection, identity binding, preparation, invocation, validation, hint consumption, refresh, and fallback. It replaces no required review. Its plausible benefit is less propagation or downstream rewriting of an overstatement, or less repeated support-resolution effort.

**Defeat criteria.**

- Supported packets become available only after ordinary support resolution.
- Deterministic access produces the same useful resolution with lower complete work.
- The author and reviewer repeat unchanged work despite the hint.
- Hints induce mistaken edits, unnecessary hedges, questions, or stops.
- The candidate fails any accepted critical case in its supported scope.

A native baseline mistake is unnecessary to investigate this hypothesis. Unresolved work before reuse can establish an opportunity. It cannot establish an intervention effect.

### Hypothesis B: deterministic preparation removes publication reconciliation

**Consumer and output.** Supply a current candidate-bound worklist containing exact relationships, the separate review association, direct access to both changed texts, and the supplied review/instruction sources. Select changed paths mechanically from the explicit comparison. Preserve verbatim source text and links; do not infer obligations from map absence.

**Timing.** Produce it after the ordinary publication candidate and applicable records are selected, before the publisher writes the coverage conclusion. Refresh on changed candidate or record bytes.

**Use.** The publisher inspects the changed README and added document, interprets the recorded review’s scope, and writes a bounded conclusion. Exact counts and digests stay deterministic. No Jev or Decisions call is needed for those relationships.

**Uncertainty and failure.** Unsupported comparison kinds, unavailable records, and failed acquisition remain explicit. Fall back to ordinary inspection. Do not convert unavailable acquisition into absent membership.

**Work added and potentially removed.** This adds producer maintenance, reads, hashing, rendering, refresh, and consumption. It might replace manual cross-referencing and repeated evidence-tool navigation. Semantic interpretation and required review remain.

**Defeat criteria.**

- The ordinary current report already supplies the same useful evidence with equal or less complete work.
- Record/range selection dominates the work and the producer does not reduce it.
- The bundle obscures changed texts or encourages unsupported review demands.
- Correctness improves only on constructed cases while native work remains unchanged.

This is an evidence-delivery hypothesis. It should not be described as assigning publication coverage to Jev or qualifying the existing jev-axi payload.

### Hypothesis C: omit the additions and retain ordinary work

Ordinary adjacent source reading, a current publication explanation, and required review may already settle both purposes. Omission retains their actual costs; it is not a zero-cost arm. If there is no remaining early claim judgment or recurring publication reconciliation for an addition to improve, omission is the supported disposition for this scope. [Claim preparation, lines 100–114 and 159][claim-preparation]; [coverage preparation, lines 119–160][coverage-preparation].

## Recommended discriminating observation

Observe **one next genuine research-document task**, with its ordinary authoring and publication preparation, at two existing checkpoints. Select the bounded section prospectively from task scope, before seeing whether it contains a defect. Preserve the accepted one-assertion/one-or-two-source producer boundary.

### Authoring checkpoint

At the first existing immutable draft checkpoint before required final review:

1. Record the designated assertion, ordinary enclosing context, citations, and their availability.
2. Record whether ordinary work has already settled support, remains unresolved, or cannot yet supply the producer’s inputs.
3. Apply deterministic preparation only to eligible inputs; account for all work required to form its case specification.
4. Record subsequent ordinary source reads, edits, reuse of the assertion, and final-review findings.

Use only prospective task-owned records needed for this observation. Do not reconstruct private history or insert a known wrong assertion.

The crucial observation is the interval between **producer-ready inputs**, **ordinary support resolution**, and **downstream reuse**. If readiness follows resolution, the current early semantic integration has no remaining judgment at that checkpoint. If readiness precedes resolution and ordinary work then spends material effort resolving or revising the claim, it supplies a concrete opportunity for a later intervention comparison.

### Publication checkpoint

When the ordinary candidate and relevant review records are selected:

1. Preserve the explicit comparison kind, identities, record-selection work, and current ordinary explanation.
2. Record manual reconciliation, source navigation, changed-text inspection, and the final bounded conclusion.
3. Identify which of those actions a mechanical evidence bundle could replace, while retaining the interpretation and review that still run.

Do not infer the actual outgoing range from the worked parent-to-child case. If the native task requires another comparison kind, record the existing producer as unavailable for that input boundary.

### Decision after the observation

- **No remaining judgment or reconciliation:** retain ordinary work and omit the additions for the observed scope.
- **Early claim opportunity:** prepare a focused prospective consumer comparison of ordinary work, deterministic preparation, and added typed judgment. Preserve Jev, Decisions, and relevant ordinary profiles; resolve candidate feasibility before intervention.
- **Recurring mechanical reconciliation:** prepare the deterministic delivery comparison, with the ordinary current report as the baseline.
- **Unsupported inputs:** narrow the producer or obtain the specific missing acquisition fact before model testing.

One task supplies a bounded observation, not a frequency estimate, calibrated rule, or adoption result. A positive result justifies focused preparation of the next comparison.
