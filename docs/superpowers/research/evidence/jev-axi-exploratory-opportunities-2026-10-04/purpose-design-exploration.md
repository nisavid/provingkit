# Opportunities across the jev-axi catalog

## Evidence and scope

The frozen records establish accepted purposes and acceptance bars, not catalog-wide utility. The brief reports bounded no-advance results for supervision and commit-message assessment and eight Stop boundaries without a demonstrated missed requirement or removable review step. Missing original records limit that Stop observation. The mandatory-note correction concerned PostToolUse. None of these findings defeats an alternate purpose.

Everything proposed below is a hypothesis. Jev supplies typed judgments; jev-axi selects context, places calls, and delivers behavior. A useful integration need not call Jev. Purpose, event placement, implementation, and authority require separate decisions. Cybersecurity efficacy, adversarial resistance, and enforcement design remain with separately owned research.

## Seven family opportunities

### SessionStart: a task-relevant capability shortlist

**Opportunity:** Help the agent choose an available capability without repeatedly surveying its environment. Startup is only one possible delivery point; task changes or first relevant use may be better.

**Inputs and consumer:** The acting agent needs the current task, actual capability descriptions, bounded readiness evidence, its existing knowledge, and evidence freshness.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary discovery; deterministic task-to-capability mappings; retrieval when needed; omit the shortlist. Semantic matching is plausible only where stable mappings leave ambiguity.

**Benefit:** Less expensive-model discovery and fewer unsuccessful tool choices.

**Defeat:** The shortlist repeats known information, becomes stale, or costs more attention than discovery.

**Cheapest observation:** In existing startup records, identify a concrete discovery sequence and whether the proposed information would have resolved it before its first consequential choice. This establishes an opportunity, not removed work.

### PreToolUse: reconcile action scope with current intent

**Opportunity:** Detect ordinary scope mistakes, such as preparing an operation for the wrong task-owned destination, and provide a precise discrepancy for the acting agent to resolve. Explore utility separately from permission or blocking behavior.

**Inputs and consumer:** The proposing agent needs the intended outcome, amendments, explicit target, proposed action, relevant state, and already completed checks.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary planning; deterministic target and argument checks; explicit operation previews; omission. Prefer detecting a factual mismatch before asking a semantic question.

**Benefit:** Avoid rework or repeated expensive reconsideration of consequential operations.

**Defeat:** Context cannot distinguish a mistake from an intended exception, or the ordinary agent already resolves the discrepancy before execution.

**Cheapest observation:** Trace an existing corrected scope mistake back to the first point at which its discrepancy was knowable. Determine whether that information existed before the tool call. Security claims are excluded.

### PostToolUse: select the next evidence-gathering move

**Opportunity:** Recognize when another similar attempt is unlikely to add information and suggest a different observation, rather than issuing a generic progress verdict or mandatory question.

**Inputs and consumer:** The executing agent needs the current subgoal, attempts and outcomes, hypotheses already eliminated, available alternatives, and known uncertainty.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary debugging; deterministic repeated-error detection and retry limits; an explicit uncertainty checklist; omission.

**Benefit:** Fewer redundant tool calls and expensive reasoning cycles.

**Defeat:** Repetition is purposeful, alternatives are unavailable, or building the history consumes the savings.

**Cheapest observation:** Examine an existing repeated-attempt sequence and name the earliest distinguishable alternative observation. A suggestion without a feasible next action does not qualify.

### Stop: three different jobs worth separating

**1. Requirement-to-evidence reconciliation.** Strengthen the accepted completion purpose by checking whether each current completion claim has sufficient, applicable evidence, including tests where required. The completing agent consumes the current request and amendments, claims, change identity, required checks, and their results. Ordinary final review, a deterministic obligation checklist, and omission are comparators. Potential benefit is catching unsupported completion or replacing an observed reconciliation step. Defeat occurs when obligations cannot be recovered reliably, evidence is stale, or ordinary review already catches the issue. The cheapest observation is a manual mapping of one retained completion claim to its requirement and evidence, identifying exactly what a candidate could add. This needs no presumption of an existing failure.

**2. Route and schedule remaining work.** Treat the boundary as an opportunity to choose among a focused review, obtaining a missing observation, preparing a handoff, or doing nothing. The coordinator or executing agent consumes current intent, completion-versus-pause meaning, uncertainty, available workers and capabilities, existing review coverage, and resource constraints. Ordinary planning, deterministic dispatch from explicit missing evidence, and omission are comparators. Benefit could come from avoiding blanket reviews and reserving expensive-model attention for unresolved questions. Defeat occurs when routing itself requires the same expensive deliberation, unavailable capacity prevents the next step, or a skipped review reduces quality. Cheapest observation: map existing boundary decisions to their actual next actions and identify a decision that simpler scheduling could make. No automatic dispatch or new authority is proposed here.

**3. Prepare a resumable state.** At a deliberate pause or handoff, select the unresolved facts, completed evidence, and next decision that another agent will need. The receiving agent consumes current task scope, latest state, attempted approaches, unresolved questions, and references to evidence. Ordinary handoff writing, deterministic extraction from structured task state, and omission are comparators. Benefit is less rediscovery and fewer repeated attempts after resumption. Defeat occurs when the handoff is unused, duplicates existing state, or omits a consequential uncertainty. Cheapest observation: inspect an existing handoff and resumption pair for actual rediscovery, then identify the smallest missing information. Stop may be the wrong event; explicit handoff or pause detection could be better.

These are completion assurance, work allocation, and continuity purposes. They need separate claims and consumers; none implies a mandatory Stop verdict.

### Pre-commit: select a focused staged-change review

**Opportunity:** Identify a consequential semantic question in the staged change and direct the agent's existing review toward it, rather than assess every generic quality dimension.

**Inputs and consumer:** The committing agent needs exact staged content, intended behavior, relevant callers or contracts, available tests, and prior findings.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary staged review; deterministic linters and test selection; a change-category checklist; omission.

**Benefit:** Better review focus or less broad expensive review, if actual review work is displaced.

**Defeat:** Partial staging hides required context, the question is generic, or another review already answered it.

**Cheapest observation:** Map an existing actionable review finding to the staged evidence and determine whether a specific review question would have exposed it. Credential efficacy remains separately owned.

### Commit-msg: clarify the committed change before describing it

**Opportunity:** Offer a concise behavior-and-scope outline from which the committing agent can write an accurate message. This is authoring support, distinct from the accepted misleading-message check.

**Inputs and consumer:** The message author needs staged changes, intended behavior, task scope, and any existing draft.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary message writing; deterministic conventions or templates; omission. Retain semantic checking as a separate hypothesis.

**Benefit:** Reduce actual authoring deliberation or corrections without demanding exhaustive enumeration.

**Defeat:** Preparing the outline duplicates understanding the agent already has, or it invents a behavioral claim.

**Cheapest observation:** Inspect an existing message-writing sequence for material reconstruction of scope. The commit-msg event may be too late; a commit-preparation route may fit.

### Pre-push: reconcile obligations across the actual push

**Opportunity:** Consolidate unresolved operational obligations across the pushed range, accounting for earlier completed checks and findings.

**Inputs and consumer:** The pushing agent needs actual refs and range, applicable project obligations, current evidence, and earlier review dispositions.

**Alternatives:** Ordinary release review; deterministic manifests and CI checks; omission.

**Benefit:** Catch a concrete missing action or remove repeated cross-commit review.

**Defeat:** No applicable obligation exists, range coverage is incomplete, or the output repeats an earlier finding.

**Cheapest observation:** Map one existing push to its applicable obligations and evidence, including whether an earlier stage already resolved each item.

## Shared components

- **Context selection:** Hypothesize smaller, sufficient evidence bundles for each consumer. Compare ordinary context, deterministic dependency selection, and no added bundle. Inspect whether a proposed judgment can be resolved from its supplied inputs; missing premises defeat it.
- **Cadence:** Trigger on changed uncertainty or evidence, not merely event count. Compare ordinary continuation, deterministic change triggers, and omission. Inspect whether consecutive opportunities contain new decision-relevant information.
- **Routing:** Use deterministic outcomes where premises are explicit; reserve typed judgment for bounded ambiguity and expensive reasoning for open questions. The coordinator consumes the route. Manually classify existing decisions; indistinguishable routing and solving costs defeat savings.
- **Batching and cache:** Reuse evidence and answers only while their dependencies and consumer question remain valid. Compare independent checks and omission. Trace one amendment or partial-staging change through invalidation; stale reuse defeats the design.
- **Thresholds and abstention:** Expose missing evidence to the workflow planner without manufacturing an operator question. Compare explicit input rules and omission. Inspect what useful next action an abstention enables; displaced questions without reduced interruption are no gain.
- **Diagnostics and accounting:** A qualification owner consumes records to decide retention or identify a specific repair. Compare minimal existing records and omission. Name the decision each field changes; collect nothing merely because it may become useful.

## Decisions before qualification

Reopen Stop's purpose beyond completion checking; consider review routing for pre-commit, authoring support for commit-msg, and task-triggered discovery for SessionStart. Keep accepted purposes as comparison branches rather than silently replacing them.

The smallest consequential operator decisions are which alternate purposes merit contracts and whether their intended benefit is addition or replacement. Select output behavior and any authority separately. Then freeze supported inputs, critical contrasts, named consumers, and complete costs. Every critical case must pass without extra false interventions, questions, or stops or reduced task quality. Charge shared work once; prioritize interruptions, then constrained expensive-model capacity, with latency and money separate. Native benefit and removed work require later authorized observation.
