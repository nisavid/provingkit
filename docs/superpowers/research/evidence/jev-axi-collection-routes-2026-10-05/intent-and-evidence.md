## Temporal binding and acquisition costs

The accepted passive invocation's procedure places the prospective boundary at a captured complete-line source offset, with device/inode and byte size recorded. The result reports a 194.571095-second admission-to-source gap; intervening activity is antecedent. Future acquisition should establish the source boundary promptly after acceptance and preserve both timestamps. A boundary-crossing record, absent timestamp, or disagreement does not become prospective by inference. Older requirements can be necessary antecedents without becoming new outcomes. [Source: passive `selection-and-accounting.md`, steps 1–4; `results.md`, “Collection and accounting limits”.]

The procedure permits a 1 MiB boundary search, an 8 MiB antecedent read, and an 8 MiB appended read; the separate import ceiling is 128 spans totaling 8 MiB, a 1 MiB manifest, and 10,000 indexed records. A larger source window does not authorize a larger selection. Source replacement, excess append size, omitted fragments, malformed records, partial indexing, unknown shapes, and unjoined outputs remain explicit limitations. Source reads and tool responses must be checked separately for truncation. These are the completed invocation's documented bounds, not authorization for a second invocation. [Source: passive `selection-and-accounting.md`, steps 1–8; `README.md`, “Import an explicit selection”; `import_records.py::MAX_MANIFEST/MAX_BYTES/MAX_SOURCES/MAX_RECORDS/main`.]

Record acquisition, page traversal, selection, interpretation, reconciliation, and voluntary logging as measurement work. Elapsed tool or import duration is not active operator effort. The importer preserves selected spans; it neither discovers missing sources nor decides relevance, chronology, human origin, or consumption. Sharing a record across consumers can charge acquisition once, but requires matching scope and identity; missing standalone or marginal effort stays unknown. [Source: passive `README.md`, “Import an explicit selection”; `selection-and-accounting.md`, “Measurement routes and gaps”; `observation-contract.md`, “Costs and joins”.]

## Smallest follow-up and decisions

For a future proposal, the coordinator can first check whether a bounded intake reaches the initiating request and subsequent relevant amendments, whether returned summaries or outputs are truncated, and whether every selected verification occurrence has a retained candidate identity and result. These are proposed checks, not checks performed here. If the requirement origin lies outside the accepted bound, either propose a specifically bounded additional antecedent source or retain incomplete intent; do not silently scan full history. [Source: passive `selection-and-accounting.md`, steps 2, 7–8; maintained `comparison-contract.md`, items 3, 5–6.]

If historical check-time identities were never retained, complete historical verification is unavailable under these routes. A later proposal may observe identities already recorded by ordinary future checks; adding instrumentation or a verification rerun requires its own scope. Consumer checking, an actual decision, or genuine resumption likewise requires a new ordinary event or voluntary retrospective account, with recall uncertainty retained. Do not ask the worker to manufacture such an event. [Source: passive `observation-contract.md`, “Claims and consumers” and “Evidence and grading”; `operator-record.md`; maintained `comparison-contract.md`, items 3, 6–8.]

The operator's remaining decisions are acceptance of another observation and its workload, antecedent/page/artifact budget, and the claim permitted when intent or candidate binding is incomplete. A comparison would additionally require the owning consumer's reliability requirements and cost priorities; this route research does not choose them. No further invocation follows from the completed result. [Source: passive `README.md`, opening; `results.md`, “Preparation required before another observation”; maintained `comparison-contract.md`, items 1, 3, 7–8.]
