# Resource lifecycle

Praxis maintains this project procedure. Load this reviewed reference directly; it does not require a packaged Praxis plugin. Any later relocation must move this maintained rule and its callers together, preserving revision and adoption evidence rather than leaving competing sources.

Keep disposable resources accountable, capacity-aware, and recoverable. Preserve the evidence and current inputs an effort needs; retire replaceable resources through their existing owners when their purpose is finished.

Load this contract before creating task resources and at success, failure, cancellation, interruption, pause, supersession, handoff, recovery, and closeout. Routine allocation can load it directly without starting another orchestration conversation. Keep resource records and unfinished obligations in the existing task record or handoff, not a second ledger.

## Bind the resource and purpose

Before allocation, record the exact resource identity or the creation request that will establish it, its meaningful purpose, creator linkage, responsible owner, expected cost or growth, evidence and current inputs to retain, and intended disposition. After creation, bind the source-returned identity and observable result to that request. A path, title, attachment, or old task association alone does not establish ownership.

For an existing resource, reconcile its identity, current purpose, ownership, active users, coupled resources, retention needs, and outstanding effects before changing its disposition. Preserve shared or unknown ownership with a named owner request and clearing condition. An unavailable historical environment or an old timestamp does not authorize takeover or disposal.

For substantial allocation or another accumulating attempt, inspect the relevant headroom and the task's applicable bound. Count resources retained from earlier attempts as well as the proposed allocation. When capacity is tight or unknown, reconcile obsolete state or choose a smaller allocation through the relevant owner; settle remaining uncertainty before that allocation. Keep unrelated work eligible. Resource-specific limits remain with their owners; this contract adds no universal quota or polling schedule.

This step is complete when the allocation or existing-resource decision has a bound subject, current owner and authority, adequate capacity evidence where required, and explicit retention or reconciliation obligations for every uncertainty that affects it.

## Reassess at workflow events

At each named event, distinguish required evidence, current inputs, usable continuing workspaces, replaceable bulk state, and coupled live workers. Preserve the original attempt's result and failure evidence before replacing or retiring its resources.

Choose a disposition for each affected resource:

- Retain resources with a continuing purpose, required evidence, unsettled ownership, or unresolved effects. Record the reason, responsible owner, and the named event or condition for the next review or disposal.
- Transfer continuing resources only after the receiving owner accepts responsibility and the surviving workspace or context is verified usable for the agreed next work. Carry outstanding obligations and the return or clearing condition.
- Retire an exclusively owned resource whose purpose and retention needs are settled only through its supported operation and existing permission rules.

A proposed transfer does not discharge the creator. Before retiring the creator, reconcile every continuing worker and coupled resource. If transfer or usability cannot be verified, preserve the creator or required resources with a concrete recovery condition.

This step is complete when each affected resource has a supported disposition or a truthful unresolved result, with evidence, ownership, and the next condition preserved.

## Preserve evidence, then use the owning operation

Identify the exact requested effect, target, current grant, supported actuator, required observations, and evidence destination before invoking the owner. Verify that necessary evidence and the disposition record will survive the proposed operation. Preserve current inputs needed by continuing work.

Keep distinct effects distinct: process termination, chat archival, chat deletion, worktree retirement, and associated-file or bulk-storage disposal each need their own authority and result. Rolecasting retains dispatch and model selection; Versionkeeping retains Git and worktree mechanics; resource-specific owners retain cleanup operations and exact scratch ownership. This contract supplies no new deletion permission or cross-resource actuator.

Supported hooks or launcher wrappers may handle settled cases automatically. Before a possibly mutating operation, retain enough task-owned intent and subject evidence to reconcile an interrupted or unacknowledged result. A terminal hook alone does not cover a hard process kill. On a supported start or resume, reconcile unfinished resource obligations and observed source state before allocating a duplicate or retrying an uncertain effect.

Use source-specific readback to distinguish an operation that did not happen, a completed effect, partial completion, a failure, and an unknown result. A missing receipt is not proof that retry is safe. Preserve completed effects during recovery instead of repeating them; preserve uncertainty when the supported control cannot settle it.

This step is complete only when the requested effects have their own verified observations or recorded partial, failed, blocked, or unknown results. A stopped process or empty container list does not prove reclaimed storage. Retained bytes do not prove a usable continuing workspace.

## Preserve usable work across a planned pause

Before a planned pause or restart can make work unavailable, reconcile actual assignments and attempts with the products and inputs needed by the next authorized actions and receivers. Include useful partial or failed returns, session-embedded products, dirty source, exact manifests and reconstruction dependencies. Checkpoint acknowledgments and a summary do not establish that those bytes survive. Verify persistent originals in place where useful; retain necessary volatile bytes with their original identity and an explicit source-to-retained locator mapping. Keep unavailable originals, unexamined scope and uncertain effects distinct. Use Versionkeeping for source and workspace mechanics; exclude credentials and authenticated profiles while preserving any required fresh-binding obligation.

Record verified preservation and failures incrementally in the existing task or handoff so interruption or exhausted capacity leaves a useful partial result. Prioritize irreplaceable inputs and carry any uncovered product as an owned obligation with its recovery condition. Do not copy reconstructible bulk or confirmed duplicates merely to increase a count; preserve the inputs needed for reconstruction. Retention authorizes no disposal or archive. An enumerated set passing readback does not establish complete discovery.

At reentry, reconcile current owners, grants, answers, holds, operation outcomes and locator mappings before dependent action. The receiver verifies that the retained product and its reconstruction inputs can be consumed for the agreed next work. A changed path must not silently change frozen input identity or a reviewed request. A missing exact manifest holds the action that needs it, even when its summary survives; independent authorized work may continue. Retained data does not restore a process, authenticate a session, release a pause, or authorize replay of an uncertain operation.

This branch is complete when the named necessary products and inputs are verified usable by their receivers, or each remaining gap has an accountable owner and concrete recovery condition. Preserve that bounded coverage and the reviewed revision used; a successful reboot, copied archive, or paused-worker label is not the branch's completion evidence.

## Name and retain chats deliberately

Classify disposable checks, continuing workers, useful experiment histories, and uncertain ownership separately. Give substantive work a concise meaningful saved name linked to its creator and purpose. Read back the saved name after normalization; a successful rename call alone does not establish a useful result.

Archive useful completed histories through the supported owner. Delete an exclusively owned finished probe chat only when no evidence or recovery need remains and the current deletion rules permit it. Preserve continuing or uncertain chats with their reason, owner, and next review condition. Chat archival, chat deletion, worker termination, and file disposal never stand in for each other's verification.

## Keep semantic judgments bounded

Use deterministic facts for exact identities, capacity arithmetic, recorded ownership, permission checks, and observed effects. A separately qualified Sys1 judgment may answer a narrow semantic question over supplied evidence, such as whether a history still contains substantive work needing retention. Its uncertainty remains explicit.

Before that judgment influences retirement, its owning evaluation must establish reliability, useful effect, complete cost, and uncertainty handling for the claimed scope. A model answer cannot supply missing ownership, permission, or disposal evidence. Without an applicable qualified judgment, preserve the uncertain resource and return the specific question to its owner.

## Return and capture

Return source-bound observations or proposals through the caller's existing typed result contract. Include the contract revision used, resource identities, triggering event, owner and authority, preserved evidence, verified effects, unfinished obligations, retained resources and reasons, accepted transfers, and remaining clearing conditions. Keep every result limited to the effect and evidence actually observed.

The caller records that it loaded the reviewed contract revision, verifies the returned result, and feeds useful corrections to this maintained source. Source review, controlled behavior evidence, native disposition evidence, publication, installation or projection, consumer adoption, and demonstrated benefit remain separate claims.
