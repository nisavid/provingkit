# Threaded Conversation

Use this guidance for chat messages, threaded comments, review replies, and
status exchanges. Apply the main skill's medium and structure rules to any
standalone artifact delivered within that exchange.

## Respond To The Live Point

Open by answering what the person asked or engaging what they just said or
did. When initiating, lead with what the reader most needs. Match their
register and conversational energy while staying professional, and acknowledge
social intent when it matters. Put a finding directly before its explanation;
a concrete mechanism or code symbol helps when the reader shares that context.
For questions about impact or progress, lead with the reader-relevant outcome
and its material limits, then explain the cause or the work behind it.

Let each next point answer a likely follow-up, doubt, or challenge. Keep replies
focused on the exchange, and end on the last substantive point. First-person
observations and judgments fit when they answer the question or identify who
holds the view.

## Keep The Exchange Actionable

Give each threaded comment one coherent point and, when action is needed, one
clear ask. Split unrelated asks into separate comments. An answer,
acknowledgment, or completed-action report may need no ask. A multi-part
question or a broader status exchange may need several connected answers;
organize them for the person responding.

In a review reply, agreement can be brief: say what follows without repeating
the comment or explaining why the suggestion is sensible. Include a correction
or tradeoff when the reviewer needs it. Review-specific severity and ask forms
belong to the review writer's policy.

## Keep The Reply Proportionate

Use connected paragraphs for a substantial reply; use a list or collapsed
detail when it makes the exchange easier to follow. Avoid ceremonial topic
labels and an inventory of the work merely to show effort. Preserve enough
context for someone joining the thread to understand the point.

When a work report stops at an authorization or scope boundary, say so plainly
and distinguish it from a technical blocker. State what remains and the next
decision or action. When sending a batch of comments, let each one's substance
shape its opening and cadence; keep a shared format where it helps comparison.
