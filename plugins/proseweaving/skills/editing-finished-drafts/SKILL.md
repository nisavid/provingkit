# Editing Finished Drafts

Return the supplied draft with its defects repaired and its useful content
intact. This pass owns verification and repair of an existing draft or batch.
Leave first drafting, code review, translation, exact substitutions, and posting
approved bytes to their owners.
It composes [writing-for-people](../writing-for-people/SKILL.md), which owns the
general register, evidence rules, and ordinary post-draft edit pass. Read that
skill and the references it calls for; use its named tells instead of maintaining
another checklist here. Specialized writers retain their content and format
contracts. Editing grants no authority to send or publish.

## Establish What Must Survive

Read the draft, supplied evidence, intended recipient and medium, and any
required wording or format. Recover available context before asking for it.
If the draft is missing, request it; do not invent a replacement. When a missing
recipient or purpose would change the edit materially, ask the specific question
and hold that decision.

Mark the author's purpose, supported claims, relevant first-person experience
and judgments, open questions, intended asks, and protected spans. Keep quoted
material, literal commands, retained text, and required structure intact. A
suspected error inside a protected span needs a separate note or a decision,
not an unannounced edit. A document's headings or deliberately parallel steps
can be part of its useful form.

## Inspect The Draft Against Its Inputs

Read once as its recipient: does the opening address what they came for, and
can they understand the point without the writer's internal task history?
Choose the register for this medium. Useful first-person evidence belongs in a
document too; document structure does not need to become a conversational reply.

Before editing, locate support outside the draft for each substantive clause.
Separate a sentence's premises from its conclusion: a qualified prediction
does not establish the mechanism it describes. Retain only the supported part
or identify the missing evidence.

Check each factual assertion against the supplied evidence, including numbers,
actors, causes, timing, confidence, and commitments. Distinguish what source
inspection establishes, what was observed in a run, and what remains a prediction
or judgment. Keep constructed test conditions attached when they limit the
claim. A confident sentence in the draft is not its own corroboration; missing
evidence also does not establish the opposite.

Run the general skill's edit pass to locate named tells. Judge each occurrence
in context: a phrase inside a required quotation, a useful label, or deliberate
parallel instructions is not disposable boilerplate. Mark concrete defects,
not a quota of changes.

For a batch, compare openings, closing moves, and length or structure across
pieces as well as within each one. Repair repetitive scaffolding from each
piece's own content. Keep order, separators, distinct recipients, and required
parallel formats; similar lengths alone do not justify padding or a new ask.

## Repair And Recheck

Edit the affected spans, preserving sound wording and supported substance.
Reorder only where the reader's understanding needs it. Correct contradicted
claims from the evidence; remove unsupported incidental assertions or qualify
them only when the qualification itself is supported. If a material assertion
cannot be checked, return the supported wording with the specific unresolved
fact identified. Do not silently remove the central question to make a draft
appear complete.

Compare the edited version with both the original and its evidence. Every
changed claim must retain its support, scope, and degree of certainty; every
removed substantive point needs a concrete reason. Check that useful evidence,
intentional form, asks, and protected spans survived. Reread the repaired text
as the recipient and, for a batch, compare the pieces again. Repeat for any
remaining defect introduced or missed by the edits. If nothing needs repair,
return the draft unchanged.

## Return The Edited Draft

Follow the requested output shape. Normally return the edited text, with only
material unresolved facts or protected-span problems noted separately when
needed. A request for final text alone does not make an unsupported central
claim publishable: state the specific gap if a supported final draft cannot
answer it. Include an edit rationale only when requested or needed to explain
such a gap. Completion means the draft meets its purpose without unsupported
claims, lost substance, or unresolved defects hidden by smoother prose.
