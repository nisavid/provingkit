# General Model Tiers

Use these tiers only for work outside the three security classes after checking complete-operation Jev suitability. Select the lowest-cost proven pair preserving the assignment's judgment margin. Assess workers by task fit; use the role-specific defaults below for operational coordination and intake. The comparisons here are operator guidance and observations, not benchmark results or proof of live settings.

GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 handle ordinary demanding work. Start precise, recoverable implementation, debugging, analysis, and focused tests on a supported Sol `medium` pair when its judgment margin suffices; raise effort with task demands. Default an independent reviewer to Sol `high` when the review is consequential but lacks the exceptional high-stakes cognitive conjunction below. Prefer Claude for UI, UX, and visual design, and GPT for review. Before dispatch, when an eligible and authorized other-provider target would satisfy the preference, recommend target reconsideration to the owning delegating workflow and wait for its revised plan before selecting on that target. Do not silently change the settled worker target. An explicitly operator-fixed target or the sole eligible target remains in force; choose its proven pair and disclose the preference limit.

| Effort | Required judgment |
| --- | --- |
| `ultra` / `ultracode` | Highly parallelizable orchestration that benefits from this effort; use the form the exact target accepts. Encourage applicable adjudicated or reconciled collaborative or competitive panels, or similar workflows, with each participant selected under its own effort policy. |
| `max` | For OpenAI and Anthropic models, every gate in [effort eligibility](effort-policy.md#max-effort), including the frontier problem conjunction and adjudicated or reconciled loop/panel workflow; other providers retain their own applicable rules. |
| `xhigh` | Hard or complex, substantial unknowns, hard to reverse, or expensive mistakes; any one condition suffices. |
| `high` | Somewhat hard or complex work that is relatively well understood; the normal consequential-review starting point. |
| `medium` | Some complexity with well-understood behavior. |

Choose from the target's supported efforts, rather than assuming every model exposes the table. Apply [effort eligibility](effort-policy.md) to all tiers; Opus is eligible for `max` only under its full conjunction and exact target support. Availability alone does not justify a higher effort. Ultra/Ultracode does not require max-effort participants or the frontier problem qualification. Use it when parallel orchestration materially helps; a small serial task or a target without the exact effort support stays on another eligible effort.

## Economical frontier and task fit

Compare useful cost, latency, and capability points within the task's quality and judgment requirements:

| Pair | Selection guidance |
| --- | --- |
| GPT-6 Luna `high` | Lower end of this comparison. Exclude Luna `xhigh` and `max` from consideration. |
| GPT-6.1 Terra `low` (Light) | About Luna `max` capability at much lower cost and latency; replaces Luna `max` and `xhigh`. Use only when task fit and judgment margin suffice. |
| GPT-6.1 Terra `medium` | Similar cost and latency to Sol Light with somewhat stronger capability; replaces Sol Light. |
| GPT-6.1 Terra `high` | Useful frontier point approaching Sol `medium` capability. |
| GPT-6.1 Sol `medium` | Prefer over Terra `xhigh`; this preference does not declare Terra `xhigh` unsupported. |

Terra Low/Medium/High bridge Luna High to Sol Medium. Terra is somewhat less capable at the top end than Sonnet 5.5 but substantially cheaper. Preserve outcome quality ahead of price; these comparisons do not impose a universal ranking across unlike tasks. Use Claude Sonnet 5.5 for some-complexity work beyond Haiku/Luna that shallow cognition can resolve without sensitivity to outcome quality. Route deeper reasoning or quality-sensitive outcomes to a suitable stronger pair, ordinarily Sol or Opus.

Sonnet and Haiku are outside the named-family medium floor and ineligible for `max` under [effort eligibility](effort-policy.md).

- **Committed code:** Terra is not an ordinary authoring default. Only `medium` or `high` may fit a narrowly predictable, contained, easy assignment with a clear judgment margin; `low` is outside this exception.
- **Computer and browser use:** consider Terra strongly for speed, price, and competence; the operator observes particularly fluid browser use at `medium`. Preserve actual correctness and interaction requirements.
- **Low-poly 3D modeling and animation:** consider Terra for spatially coherent, accurate prototypes and first passes, including animation. Validate the prototype before handing it to Sol 6.1 or Opus 5.5 for elaboration, refinement, and polish. Terra is not the detail or polish lane.

Consider Claude Haiku 5.5 for simple, well-understood classification, extraction, and routing. For these bounded worker tasks, choose the lowest supported effort that preserves judgment and outcome quality. Eligible Luna and Grok pairs are also candidates when task fit permits. Grok 4.7 `high` may fit moderately complex, well-understood work within its role. Apply [effort eligibility](effort-policy.md) and [native label binding](capability-probes-and-fallbacks.md#prove-an-exact-pair) to every choice.

## Exceptional cognitive work

Outside the named role bindings above, reserve GPT-6 Astra or Claude Fable 5.1 for work that is both **high-stakes** and demands at least one of deep cognition, intuitive hypothesis, analysis or synthesis, thoughtful articulation, or judgment over substantial unknown unknowns. Complexity or a named cognitive need without high stakes stays on Sol or Opus. Astra is the more economical exceptional option; use it for a bounded assignment that preserves the high-stakes judgment, not to enlarge scope. Use Fable for choicest beneficial deep work only with a current successful [target proof](capability-probes-and-fallbacks.md#prove-fable). This general-tier conjunction does not replace the more specific Fable security fallback rule.
